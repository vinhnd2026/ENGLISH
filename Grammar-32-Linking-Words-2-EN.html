1. Reason

a) Because, as, since

As and since have the meaning of because, and they can begin a sentence.

As it was late, we decided to go home.
Since it was late, we decided to go home.

In formal, written language, for can also mean because, but it cannot begin a sentence.

Peter has given up sailing, for he doesn't have the time.

b) Because of, on account of, due to, owing to

because of / on account of / due to / owing to + noun

All of these can be used in place of because of when the reason is expressed as a noun or noun phrase.

Everyone was depressed on account of the bad weather.

Note this difference:

Sue's success was due to her hard work.
Sue succeeded owing to her hard work.
⚠️ Remember the pattern: noun + be + due to (the reason follows the verb be), while owing to follows an ordinary verb.

2. Text organizers

As well as linking words that join clauses within a sentence, there are also linking words that join ideas across sentences and paragraphs. These are sometimes called text organizers because they make clear the organization of what we say or write. They are more common in writing or formal speech. They usually come at the start of a sentence and have a comma afterwards, but they can also come after a comma in the middle of a sentence.

a) Sequencing

We often number or order the points we are making:

First (of all) …, Secondly …, Next …, Then …, Finally / lastly / last of all …

In narrative, the sequence of events can be introduced by: First …, Then …, After that …, Finally / in the end …

b) Adding

Furthermore …, Moreover …, In addition to …, As well as this …, Besides this …

c) Giving opinions

Personally …, In my own opinion / view …

d) Giving examples

For example …, For instance …

We can also use such as to give an example, but it is not used at the beginning of a sentence.

The factory produces electrical goods, such as food mixers and other kitchen appliances.

e) Showing a result

Formal ways to show a result include:

Consequently …, As a result …, Thus …

f) Making a contrast

On the other hand …, However …, Nevertheless …, In contrast …, In comparison …

g) Summarizing

In conclusion …, To sum up …
In conclusion, I would like to thank you all for your help.
Owing to the increased demand for parking spaces, the company has decided to enlarge the car park.
This radio is expensive, but on the other hand it has very good reception.

⭐ Key points

  1. As / since mean because and can begin a sentence; for also means “because” in formal writing, but it cannot begin a sentence.
  2. Because of / on account of / due to / owing to are followed by a noun; note the difference between noun + be + due to and verb + owing to.
  3. Text organizers help readers and listeners to follow the development of an argument. Most of the expressions listed are formal in use.
  4. Because text organizers make links across sentences, their meaning is only clear when you study the ideas that come before and after them — the whole context.
