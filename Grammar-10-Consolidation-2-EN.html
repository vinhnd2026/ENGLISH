🔄 Quick review — The four grammar areas in this unit

This consolidation unit brings together the four areas you have just studied: indirect speech, conditionals, wish and the passive. Read through the summaries below, then do the exercises to check yourself.

a) Indirect speech (Reported speech)

When we report what someone said with a verb in the past tense (said, told, asked…), we usually move the tense one step back, and change pronouns and time/place expressions to fit the person reporting. Yes/No questions use if/whether; questions with a question word keep the question word, and both types use statement word order (no inversion). Advice, reminders and orders are often reported with advise / remind / tell / ask + object + to V; accusing someone uses accuse somebody of + V-ing.

He said, 'I am busy.' → He said (that) he was busy.
(am → was: the tense moves one step back.)
'Did you see anything?' → They asked me if I had seen anything.
(A Yes/No question uses if, with statement word order.)

b) Conditionals

Type 2: If + Past Simple, S + would + V  ·  Type 3: If + Past Perfect, S + would have + V₃

Type 2 talks about things that are not true in the present; type 3 talks about things that did not happen in the past. Unless has an equivalent meaning to if … not.

If the weather hadn't been so bad, I would have ridden my bike.
(A type 3 conditional.)
We won't go out unless the weather is good.
(unless = if … not)

c) Wish · It's time · I'd rather

wish + Past Simple (present) · wish + Past Perfect (past) · wish + would (wanting a change) · It's time + S + Past Simple · I'd rather + S + Past Simple

Wish expresses something contrary to the facts, or a regret. After It's (high) time and after I'd rather (talking about someone else's actions) we use the past simple even though the meaning is present.

I wish I had known about that.
(wish + Past Perfect: regretting something in the past.)
It's time we went home. I'd rather you didn't open the window.

d) Passives

be + V₃  ·  It is said that … ↔ S is said to V / to have V₃  ·  have/get + O + V₃

The passive is used when the action is more important than the person who does it, or when that person is unknown or need not be named. With reporting verbs (say, think, believe…) there are two parallel structures. The have something done structure is used when we arrange for someone to do a job (or when someone does something to us).

My bike had been stolen. The thieves are thought to have put the bikes in a van.
We had our house painted last month.
(have something done)

⭐ Key points

  1. Indirect speech: move the tense back when the reporting verb is in the past; Yes/No questions use if/whether, question-word questions keep the question word — both use statement word order.
  2. advise / remind / tell / ask + object + to V report advice, reminders and orders; accuse somebody of + V-ing makes an accusation; notice the difference between say (that)… and tell somebody (that)….
  3. Type 3 conditionals are for things not true in the past: If + Past Perfect, would have + V₃; unless = if … not.
  4. wish + Past Perfect regrets a past event; wish + would complains or wants a change; It's time + Past Simple; I'd rather + S + Past Simple.
  5. Reporting passives: It is thought that… ↔ S is thought to have + V₃ when the event happened earlier; have/get + O + V₃ for jobs done by someone else.
