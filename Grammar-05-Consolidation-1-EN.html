🔁 Review — What does this unit cover?

This is a consolidation unit: there is no new theory. The exercises below mix together everything from the previous four units — try to recall each point before you begin:

  • Past time (Grammar 1): Past Simple · Past Continuous · Past Perfect · used to · participle clauses (Before/After/While + V-ing).
  • Present perfect (Grammar 2): Present Perfect Simple & Continuous · for / since · ever, already, yet · “It's ages since…”.
  • Future time (Grammar 3): will · going to · the Present Continuous for fixed arrangements · the Future Perfect (will have + V₃) with by the time…
  • Present time (Grammar 4): Present Simple contrasted with Present Continuous · state verbs.

Three typical patterns you will meet again in the exercises:

By the time we get to the theatre, the play will have begun.
I've been working here for three years.
While I was having dinner, the phone rang.

⭐ Key points

  1. Sentence transformations (Exercises 1 and 3): use between two and five words, do not change the word given, and keep the original meaning exactly.
  2. By the time + present simple → the main clause usually uses the Future Perfect: will have + V₃ (the event will be complete before that future point).
  3. for + a period of time (for three years); since + the starting point (since 2020); the pattern It's ages since + present perfect…
  4. Two past actions at the same time: the longer action in progress → Past Continuous; the shorter, interrupting action → Past Simple (While I was having dinner, the phone rang).
  5. When one past event happened before another: the event that happened first uses the Past Perfect (When we arrived, David had gone home).
  6. A future plan that is fixed and arranged → Present Continuous (We're having a meeting tomorrow); a prediction based on visible signs → going to (It's going to rain).
