1. Jobs and workplaces

cashier — bank · cook — restaurant / kitchen · dentist — surgery · farmer — field · hairdresser — salon
librarian — library · mechanic — garage · miner — coal-mine · musician — concert hall / studio · photographer — studio
pilot — cockpit · porter — hotel · receptionist — office · vicar — church · waiter — restaurant

A cashier handles money in a bank or shop. A cook prepares food (a chef is the senior cook in charge of a kitchen). A dentist treats your teeth at a surgery (a doctor's or dentist's office). A farmer works the land; a hairdresser cuts hair in a salon; a librarian works in a library; a mechanic repairs cars in a garage; a miner digs coal underground; a photographer works in a studio; a pilot flies the plane from the cockpit; a porter carries bags in a hotel or station; a receptionist greets visitors in an office; a vicar is a priest in a church; a waiter serves food in a restaurant.

More jobs

accountant · carpenter · chef · estate agent · firefighter · plumber · refuse collector · vet

An accountant keeps financial records; a carpenter makes things from wood; an estate agent helps people buy and sell houses; a firefighter puts out fires; a plumber repairs water pipes; a refuse collector collects rubbish; a vet treats animals.

Yesterday I had to give an injection to an injured bull.
(a vet speaking)
I'll come round and replace all the pipes in the kitchen.
(a plumber speaking)

2. work · job · business · living

make a living · go to work · own business · without work · cost of living · none of your business · a good job · work to do · workmen · a work of art

To make a living is to earn enough money to live. Work (uncountable) is the general activity; a job (countable) is one specific post. Business can mean a company or commercial activity — and in none of your business it means “your private affair”. The cost of living is how expensive everyday life is. Workmen are manual workers, and a work (of art) is a piece created by an artist.

Jack makes his living working as a journalist.
Stop interfering! This is none of your business.
An early work by Picasso was sold for £3,000,000.

3. Phrasal verbs at work

go over · come up with · fall through · fill in · get on with · turn down · face up to · draw up · take over · call off

go over something = check it carefully; come up with an idea = think of it; a deal falls through = it fails and does not happen; fill in a form = write the information in the spaces; get on with somebody = have a good relationship with them; turn down an offer = refuse it; face up to a fact = accept a difficult truth; draw up a contract = prepare it in writing; take over a company = gain control of it; call off a meeting or party = cancel it.

Our deal with the Chinese company may fall through.
A multinational company is trying to take over our firm.

4. Getting a job and word formation

promotion · salary · advertisement · application · qualifications · references · appointment · dismiss

A promotion is a move to a better job in the same company. Your salary is the fixed pay you receive monthly or yearly. Jobs are announced in an advertisement; you send in an application and list your qualifications (exams and skills) and references (letters from people who know your work). An appointment is the act of choosing someone for a post. To dismiss someone is to sack them.

Common word families

advertise → advertising · resign → resignation · economy → economic · employ → unemployed / employee / employer · apply → application · profit → profitable · earn → earnings · retire → retirement
If you work hard, the company will give you a promotion.
She accepted my resignation but warned me about the economic situation.

⭐ Key points

  1. job is countable (a job, two jobs); work meaning “employment in general” is uncountable — never *a work in that sense; a work means a piece of art.
  2. salary = pay calculated monthly/yearly (typically office jobs); wages = pay calculated hourly/weekly (typically manual jobs); income = money received in general; earnings = the total amount a person earns.
  3. be in charge = be responsible; hand in your notice = formally tell your employer you are leaving; be sacked = be dismissed; expenses = work costs the company pays back.
  4. Core phrasal verbs: face up to (accept a hard truth) · fall through (fail) · call off (cancel) · fill in (complete a form) · take over (gain control) · draw up (prepare a document) · come up with (think of).
  5. People in a company: the employer hires you; the employee is the person hired; the staff are all the employees together.
