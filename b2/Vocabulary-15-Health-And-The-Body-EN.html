1. Parts of the body

a) Head and face

cheek · chin · lips · nose · throat · neck · beard

Head and face words run through the whole unit: hold your nose, a kiss on the cheek, a sore throat.

b) Arms, body and legs

wrist · thumb · fist · elbow · waist · back · spine · knee · ankle · heel · toe

Note: spine is the more formal word for the backbone; brains (plural) can also mean intelligence; heart can also mean the centre of a place (the heart of the countryside).

One of the boxers punched the other on the chin and knocked him out.
Norma wears a heart on a gold chain around her neck.

2. Illness, pain and treatment

ache · pain · agony · hurt · injured · bruised · swollen · heal · cure · treat · examine · suffer from · recover / get over · surgery · ward · prescription · ambulance · stretcher · injection · operation · temperature

An ache is a long, dull pain, often combined with a body part (backache, toothache); pain is the general noun; agony is very great pain. hurt means injure or be painful; injured suggests an accident; bruised = black and blue; swollen = made larger by injury or illness.

Wounds heal; doctors cure diseases and treat patients; a doctor examines you to find out what is wrong. To get better after an illness is to recover (from) it or get over it; suffer from + illness means to have that illness.

George's cut arm took over a week to heal.
Martin hasn't quite got over his illness yet.
I was in agony all night with earache.

3. Body idioms

one's heart is in the right place · hold one's tongue · jaw away · have a lot of cheek · can't stomach · back someone · poke one's nose in · thumb a lift · put one's foot in it · one's heart aches

These idioms cannot be understood word by word: his heart is in the right place = he is kind; hold his tongue = say nothing; jawed away = talked non-stop; have a lot of cheek = be rude and disrespectful; can't stomach = cannot bear (a person or thing); back his boss = support him; poke his nose in = interfere in other people's business; thumb a lift = hitchhike; put his foot in it = say the wrong thing; his heart ached = he was homesick.

Paul held his tongue and said nothing.
Graham thumbed a lift to work.

⭐ Key points

  1. An ache (dull, lasting; toothache, backache) differs from pain in general and agony (extreme pain).
  2. hurt / injured / bruised / swollen: be painful or slightly injured / injured (in an accident) / black and blue / swollen up.
  3. heal = (a wound) becomes healthy again; cure = make an illness go away; treat = give medical care; examine = look at a patient carefully.
  4. Getting better: recover from / get over + illness; having an illness: suffer from + illness.
  5. Fixed phrases: take someone's temperature, be operated on, come round (regain consciousness), go down with (catch an illness), wear off (lose its effect).
  6. Body idioms must be learnt with their figurative meaning, not word by word.
