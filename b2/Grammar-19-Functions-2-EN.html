1. The functions practised in this lesson

This lesson practises another set of communicative functions, along with the appropriate response for each one:

  • Things to say on special occasions: Congratulations! · Well done! · Bless you! · Cheers!
  • Asking for and giving an opinion: What did you think of the film, then?
  • Pausing to think: Wait a minute, let me see.
  • Asking for permission: Would it be all right if I left a bit early today?
  • Giving and refusing permission: Sure, go ahead. / You may not.
  • Expressing preferences: I think I'd rather have fish, actually.
  • Promising: I'll definitely bring your camera back tomorrow.
  • Reassuring: Don't worry, everything will turn out all right.
  • Recommending: I recommend the Hilton.
  • Refusing to do something: Sorry, no, I won't do it.
  • Making and responding to a request: Could you possibly turn on the air conditioning?
  • Suggesting: Why don't we have a party next weekend?
  • Thanking: That's very kind of you, I appreciate it.

— Said when someone has just achieved something good.
Bless you!
— The familiar phrase said when someone sneezes.
What did you think of the film, then?
— Asking for an opinion.
Wait a minute, let me see.

2. Responding appropriately

Each function usually comes with a suitable kind of response. Responding in the wrong "way" sounds very strange, even if the grammar is correct: for example, when you are introduced to someone and hear "How do you do", the correct reply is "How do you do" — not "Very well, thank you". Similarly, when refusing a request, "I can't, I'm afraid" usually sounds softer and more suitable than "No, I won't".

Hear the function → respond with the matching function, at the right level of politeness
How kind, you really shouldn't have bothered. — It was nothing, really.
— Responding to thanks when receiving a gift.
I can't stop worrying about my exam tomorrow. — I'm sure you'll do well.
— Reassuring someone who is worried.
Would you mind moving your bag from the seat? — Oh, sorry.
— Responding to a request with an apology and an action.
Is it all right if I use your bike? — Sure, go ahead.
— Giving permission, informally.
Do you want beer or wine? — I'd prefer beer, please.
— Expressing a preference with I'd prefer.

⭐ Key points

  1. How we choose our words depends on the situation, on our relationship with the person we are talking to, and on the subject of the conversation.
  2. Respond appropriately — informally or politely — based on those factors, and on how the other person spoke to us first.
  3. If we fail to use a polite form when one is needed, the listener may think we are rude, and the conversation may not achieve the result we want.
