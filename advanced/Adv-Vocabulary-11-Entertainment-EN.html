1. Popular music and classical music: two worlds

The first two texts of this unit explore the relationship between popular music and classical music. A distinction is a clear difference between two things: a distinction between popular music and classical music. Entertainment aimed at the widest audience is mass entertainment. From that point, popular music branched away — split off and developed in its own direction — until it became incompatible with (unable to exist or work together with) the classics. The phrase in some respects means "in some ways".

The second text introduces a new breed of classical musicians — a new type or generation of performer. They have achieved the status (position and prestige) of rock stars, yet they do not wish to be associated with (be linked in people's minds with) the lifestyle of the traditional classical audience. This is a generation raised on rock (brought up listening to rock) that is now settling into (entering and getting comfortable in) affluent middle-age.

There is a clear distinction between popular music and classical music.
(distinction — a clear difference between two things)
The new singers have achieved the status of rock stars.
(status — the position or prestige someone has)

2. Theatre and film collocations

Many theatre nouns go with one fixed partner word — choosing the near-synonym sounds wrong at once:

· come on stage: walk onto the theatre stage (not screen — a screen is for film).
· give a performance: perform (the fixed noun here is performance, not acting).
· interval: the break in the middle of a play (interlude is a short piece played between acts).
· amateur dramatic society: a local amateur theatre group.
· on the edge of one's seat: gripped by suspense.
· dress rehearsal: the final rehearsal in full costume.
· scenery: the painted sets on stage (landscape is natural scenery outdoors).
· reviews: critics' written judgements (previews are advance showings).
· horror films: frightening films.

The actors came on stage to loud applause.
(come on stage — walk onto the stage at the start of a performance)
I thought it was a good film but it got terrible reviews.
(reviews — the judgements written by critics)

3. People in entertainment and the orchestra

a) Performers

· clown: makes people laugh at the circus.
· vocalist: a singer.
· cast: all the actors in a play or film.
· juggler: entertains by throwing and catching things.
· acrobat: performs gymnastics.
· understudy: an actor who learns a part and takes over in an emergency.
· conductor: the person who directs an orchestra.
· stuntman: performs dangerous actions in place of an actor.
· ballerina: a female ballet dancer in a leading role.

b) The orchestra and the concert

An orchestra has four main sections: string (violins, cellos…), woodwind (clarinets, flutes…), brass (trumpets…) and percussion (drums…). Also: lyrics (the words of a song), organist (the person who plays the organ), chorus (the part of a song everyone joins in with), opera (a play set to music), concert (a musical performance).

The conductor raised his baton and the orchestra began to play.
(conductor — the person who tells an orchestra what to do)
The string section of the orchestra needs a new violinist.
(string section — the players of stringed instruments)

4. Word formation: common suffixes

The word-formation text in this unit shows the typical patterns: REHEARSE → rehearsal (the suffix -al makes a noun), ENERGY → energetically (adjective energetic + -ally makes an adverb), THINK → thoughts, SPEAK → speakers (-er for the person), LAUGH → laughter (an irregular form to learn separately), BACK → background (a compound: someone's past experience and training), NATIONAL → nationalities (-ity, plural y → ies), NIGHT → overnight (in the space of one night), CREATE → creative (-ive makes an adjective), APPEAR → appearances (-ance makes a noun).

Dancers of all shapes and sizes begin to tumble energetically through the doors.
(energetically — in a lively, active way, from ENERGY)
The group became overnight celebrities.
(overnight — suddenly, in a very short time, from NIGHT)

5. Games, prepositions and multiple meanings

a) Games and pastimes

jigsaw puzzle (look at the picture on the box to see where the pieces go), cards (when you deal, you get three aces), billiards (the white ball hits the red into the corner pocket), draughts (take all the opponent's pieces in one move and sweep the board), television (pass the remote control), board game (throw the dice and pick up a card), darts (the bulls-eye is worth fifty), table tennis (if the ball hits the net on a serve, it doesn't count), chess (put her in check with your queen), video (don't forget to rewind it when it finishes).

b) Fixed prepositions

on stilts · packed with people · world record for (the best in a particular activity) · over the loudspeakers · a craze for something · appear in the role of · tickets for something · on the piano · booed off the stage · challenge someone to a game.

c) One word, several meanings

record: a new album / a criminal past (criminal record) / a written account that is kept. attention: media interest (media attention) / someone's interest and admiration / brought to my attention (reported to me). presence: an imposing bearing / presence of mind (calm quick thinking) / being present in a place (a police presence). scene: a part of a play / the kind of thing someone enjoys (not my scene) / the place where something happened. release: a new record that comes out / being set free / relief from something (a release of pain).

David challenged Cathy to a game of chess.
(challenge someone to — invite someone to compete in)
There was a huge police presence at the football match.
(presence — the fact of being in a place, here of the police)

⭐ Key points

  1. Theatre collocations: come on stage · give a performance · interval · dress rehearsal · scenery · reviews · horror films — avoid the near-misses: screen, acting, interlude, landscape, previews.
  2. The four sections of the orchestra: string · woodwind · brass · percussion; lyrics = the words of a song, chorus = the part everyone joins in with.
  3. Performers: a conductor directs the orchestra · an understudy takes over in an emergency · a stuntman does the dangerous scenes · an acrobat performs gymnastics · a juggler throws and catches · the cast is the whole group of actors.
  4. Word formation: rehearsal, speakers, creative, appearances follow regular suffix patterns; learn separately laughter (LAUGH), background (BACK), overnight (NIGHT), nationalities (NATIONAL).
  5. Fixed prepositions: packed with · a craze for · tickets for · on the piano · booed off the stage · challenge someone to a game.
  6. Multiple meanings: record, attention, presence, scene, release — one word serves three different contexts; learn each with its example phrase.
