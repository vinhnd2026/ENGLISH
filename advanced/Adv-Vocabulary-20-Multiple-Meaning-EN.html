1. Phrasal verbs with pull — one verb, many meanings

When pull combines with different particles, it forms phrasal verbs with completely different meanings. Often the whole phrase can be replaced by a single, more formal verb.

pull away = start moving · pull a muscle = damage it · pull out = produce / extract · pull back = open · pull off = succeed · pull up = tell off / stop

· pull away: a vehicle starts to move (started moving).
· pull a muscle: damage or strain a muscle (damaged).
· pull out a gun: take it out and show it (produced); pull out a tooth: remove it (extracted).
· pull back the curtains: open them (opened).
· pull it off: succeed in something difficult (succeeded).
· pull out troops: remove them from a place (withdrew).
· pull someone up: reprimand them (told off).
· pull something along: drag it (dragged).
· pull up (a vehicle): stop (stopped).

The lorry pulled away very slowly because of its heavy load.
(pull away = started moving)
Mike was pulled up by his boss for making a joke about the Chairman.
(pull up = told off, reprimanded)

2. run — when it works and when it doesn't

run has many different meanings. Some of the uses below are correct and very common, but others sound plausible and are simply not what native speakers say.

a) Correct uses

· run someone to a place: take them there by car.
· your eyes run: your eyes water (because of chlorine, smoke, etc.).
· run a train / a service: operate or provide it.
· has … to run: is still valid (a contract with six months left).
· run for (the Presidency): stand as a candidate.

b) Common incorrect uses

· Passing on a message: use pass / give — not run your message.
· Kept in records: use recorded / written — not run in our records.
· A total cost: use be — the cost will be 50% higher, not run.
· If absolutely necessary: at a pinch / at a push — not at a run.
· The truth of the situation: the fact of the matter — not the run of the matter.

Would you like me to run you to the bus station?
(run = take by car)
My contract still has six months to run.
(run = still be valid)

3. One word completes a whole family of fixed phrases

Some words fit a whole group of fixed phrases. Learning the phrases as a set is easier than learning them one by one.

panel: an instrument panel · a panel of experts · a control panel · a wooden panel
secret: a secret ballot · a secret agent · keep it a secret · meet in secret · the secret of success
stock: take stock of the situation · out of stock · the stock exchange · the stock market
time: a time sheet · a time zone · only time will tell · long time no see · for the time being
child: a child minder · child abuse · child care facilities · a child prodigy · behaving like a child
They decided to meet in secret.
(meet in secret)
Only time will tell.
(only time will tell)

4. odd — strange, unmatched, occasional

a) Correct uses

· odd = strange: odd characters; It's odd to think that…
· odd = not forming a pair: odd socks (two socks that do not match).
· the odd … = occasional: the odd flash of genius.
· the odd one out: the one that is different from the rest of a group.

b) Common incorrect uses

· One more of something: one more glass — not one odd glass.
· Compatible: compatible with — not odd with.
· A bad feeling about something: an ill wind — not an odd wind.
· The pointing finger: index finger — not odd finger.
· Steps that are too quick: fast — not odd.

Look I can't wear odd socks - everyone will laugh at me.
(odd = not matching, not a pair)
The match was mediocre - apart from the odd flash of genius from Lupeto.
(the odd = the occasional)

5. Collocation traps — only two of the three words fit

For each gap there are often three words that all look possible, but only two really collocate with the rest of the sentence. The third word is the trap.

· keep a receipt: retain / keep (not maintain)
· stand still: stand / stay still (not stop)
· the last part of a programme: final part / programme (not series)
· a light skin condition: mild / slight (not weak)
· meaning in life: purpose / direction (not aim)
· impudence: cheek / nerve (not brain)
· become twice the size: swelled / grew (not rose)
· until the very end: the very / bitter end (not far)
· short of time: pushed / pressed for time (not tight)
· your turn: your turn / go (not take)

Please retain this receipt, as it means we can identify your photographs more quickly.
(retain / keep a receipt)
Today's not a good day for a meeting. I'm rather pushed for time.
(pushed / pressed for time)

6. One word replacing three different words

A single word can replace three different bold words in three sentences, because that word itself has several meanings. The context tells you which meaning is being used.

· take: require (it takes strength) · accept (hard to take) · steal (take = steal).
· stretch: not be fully challenged (be stretched) · become bigger (the pullover stretched) · reach out (stretch out your arm).
· propose: intend (propose to leave) · nominate (propose someone for a post) · suggest (propose setting up a meeting).
· space: room to work at · a storage area · a place to sit — all mean an empty area.
· moment: a short while (thought for a moment) · a point in time (from that moment on) · the last possible time (at the last moment).

It would take a lot of strength to lift that boulder.
(take = require)
At the last moment, they decided to pull out of the competition.
(moment = point in time)

⭐ Key points

  1. Learn phrasal verbs with pull as whole phrases: each particle gives a completely different meaning (pull away, pull off, pull up…), and many can be replaced by a single verb such as withdraw, extract, succeed.
  2. run is correct for: taking someone by car, eyes watering, operating a service, remaining valid, and standing for election. Do not use run for messages, records or total costs.
  3. Some words fit a whole family of fixed phrases: panel, secret, stock, time, child — learn them as sets.
  4. odd has three main meanings: strange, not matching, and occasional (the odd…). It does not mean "one more" or "compatible".
  5. When three words are offered, usually only two collocate: retain/keep a receipt, stand/stay still, pushed/pressed for time.
  6. One word can carry several meanings in different sentences (take, stretch, propose, space, moment) — use the context to choose the meaning.
