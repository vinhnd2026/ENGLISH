1. Happen, appear, succeed or fail

This unit (and the next two Phrasal verbs units) does not teach completely new phrasal verbs; it focuses on further meanings of phrasal verbs that are already familiar. The same verb can mean something quite different depending on the context. The first group is about something happening, appearing, succeeding or failing.

come about = happen · come up = occur (a problem) · crop up = happen unexpectedly · come out = appear · come off = take place successfully · fall through = fail to come to completion · catch on = become popular
Let me explain how the situation came about.
(come about = happen)
Look, something has come up, and I can't meet you.
(come up = a problem has occurred)
I'm afraid that deal didn't come off after all.
(come off = take place successfully)
The plan fell through at the last minute.
(fall through = fail to come to completion)

2. More meanings of COME

Besides the group above, come combines with several particles to form advanced meanings that are common in written English and in exams:

come down to = be in the end a matter of · come in for = receive (especially criticism, blame) · come up against = meet a difficulty · come up to = equal (expectations, a standard) · come up with = think of (an answer, a plan, a solution)
It all comes down to whether you are prepared to accept less money.
(come down to = be in the end a matter of)
The government has come in for a lot of criticism over the decision.
(come in for = receive criticism)
The play didn't come up to expectations.
(come up to = equal expectations)
We still haven't come up with a solution to the problem.
(come up with = think of a solution)

3. BRING and CARRY

These two verbs form many phrasal verbs meaning "cause", "succeed in doing" or "complete":

bring about = cause to happen · bring off = succeed in doing something difficult · bring on = cause the onset of (an illness, trouble for oneself) · bring round = influence someone to your point of view · bring up = mention · carry off = complete successfully, despite a problem · carry out = complete a plan
The crisis was brought about by Brenda's resignation.
(bring about = cause to happen)
After much discussion, I brought the committee round to my point of view.
(bring round = persuade)
I feel I ought to bring up another small matter.
(bring up = mention)
The attack was successfully carried out.
(carry out = complete a plan)

4. BREAK and FALL

This group contains many verbs about emotions, endings and last-resort situations:

break down = lose control of the emotions · break off = stop talking · break up = come to an end (party, meeting) · fall about = laugh a lot · fall back on = use as a last resort · fall for = be deceived by; fall in love with · fall out with = quarrel with · face up to = deal bravely with (responsibilities) · feel up to = feel capable of doing · follow up = act upon (a suggestion, information)
David broke down and wept when he heard the news.
(break down = lose control of the emotions)
He broke off to answer the phone.
(break off = stop talking)
It was an unlikely story but he fell for it.
(fall for = be deceived by)
You have to face up to your responsibilities.
(face up to = deal bravely with)

5. The GET family — many advanced meanings

Get forms more phrasal verbs than any other verb; all the meanings below are far from the basic "receive / obtain":

get across = be understood (an idea) · get at = imply (about personal matters) · get down to = begin to deal seriously with · get off with = avoid punishment, receive a light sentence · get on = make progress in life · get on for = approach (an age, time, number) · get over = be surprised (can't get over) · get over with = come to the end of something unpleasant · get round to = find time to do · get up to = do (something, usually bad)
I had the feeling I wasn't getting the meaning across.
(get across = make an idea understood)
It's time we got down to some real work.
(get down to = begin seriously)
He must be getting on for seventy.
(get on for = approach an age)
Sorry, but I haven't got round to fixing the tap yet.
(get round to = find time to do)

6. The remaining verbs (A – E)

add up = make sense · ask after = inquire about · back down = yield in an argument · bargain for = take into account · bear out = confirm the truth · call up = mobilise for military service · count on = rely on · do away with = abolish; murder · do up = decorate · draw up = come to a stop (vehicle); organise (a document) · drop in = pay a visit · drop off = fall asleep · end up = finish in a certain way or place
His evidence just doesn't add up.
(add up = make sense)
Jim was asking after you.
(ask after = inquire about)
We hadn't bargained for there being so much traffic, and we missed the plane.
(bargain for = take into account)
We ended up staying there for lunch.
(end up = finish in a certain way)
⚠️ Many verbs in this unit are colloquial: catch on, crop up, drop in, drop off, do away with, get at, get up to — they suit spoken English better than formal writing.

⭐ Key points

  1. Phrasal verbs 1–3 focus on further meanings of familiar phrasal verbs: one verb can have several meanings, and the context decides which one is right.
  2. The come group: come up / crop up = occur; come off = succeed, fall through = fail; come up with = think of; come up against = meet a difficulty; come in for = receive criticism.
  3. The bring / carry group: bring up = mention; bring round = persuade; bring about = cause; carry out = complete a plan; carry off / bring off = succeed in something difficult.
  4. The get group: get down to = begin seriously; get round to = find time to do; get on for = approach; get over = be surprised; get up to = do (something bad).
  5. The fall group: fall for = be deceived / fall in love; fall back on = use as a last resort; fall out with = quarrel with someone.
  6. Watch the register: many of these verbs are colloquial — catch on, crop up, drop in, drop off, do away with, get at, get up to.
