1. Weather, climate and the environment

Writing about the natural world uses many formal collocations and fixed phrases, especially in journalism:

imminent destruction · prophets of doom · an endless cycle of change · on a global scale · go so far as to say · global warming

imminent (about to happen — used for bad things) · prophets of doom (people who always predict disaster) · cycle of change (a repeating pattern of change) · on a global scale (worldwide) · go so far as to (to say or do something extreme).

Weather words

draught (a current of cold air in a room) · downpour (a sudden heavy rain) · torrential (falling in a torrent) · drizzle (light fine rain) · lightning (the flash in a storm) · thunder (the sound after lightning) · blossom (flowers on a tree; in blossom = in flower) · horizon (the line where earth and sky meet) · the countryside (rural areas).

Some scientists go so far as to say there will never be 'natural weather' again.
(go so far as to say — an extreme claim)
I'm soaked — I got caught in a downpour.
(downpour — sudden heavy rain)
Last year this tree was struck by lightning.
(struck by lightning — hit by a lightning flash)

2. Wild animals and survival

prey (an animal hunted by another) · wildlife (wild animals in general) · species (a type of animal or plant) · breed (a variety of domestic animal) · tame (not afraid of people) · extinct (no longer existing) · young (the babies of animals, as a noun).

Conservation language

captivity (being kept in a zoo etc.; born and bred in captivity) · survival (continuing to live) · endangered species (a species in danger of dying out) · environmentalists (people who campaign to protect the environment) · volunteers (unpaid helpers) · maternal instincts (a mother's protective feelings) · abundant (plentiful) · maturity (full growth) · a handful of (very few) · diversity (variety).

Through my binoculars I watched a tiger stalking its prey.
(stalk — follow quietly in order to catch)
The white rhino is now an endangered species.
(endangered species — at risk of extinction)
Nowadays only a handful of wild crocodiles remain there.
(a handful of — very few)

3. Words associated with animals

Each animal has its own set of associated words — body parts, sounds, homes and equipment:

horse: hoof, saddle, stable, mane · bee: hive, buzz, sting · lion: cub, mane, roar · mosquito: whine, net, spray · dog: bark, kennel, lead.

sheep: flock, lamb, wool · elephant: ivory, trunk, tusks · mouse: squeak, hole, trap · bat: blind, nocturnal, wing · cat: purr, kitten, scratch.

Bees buzz around the hive.
(buzz — the humming sound bees make)
She may look fierce, but the lioness has maternal instincts like any other female animal.
(maternal instincts — a mother's natural protective feelings)

4. Confusable pairs; one word, several contexts

Choosing the right word in a pair

current / draught: a current is a flow of water or air in general; cold air coming through a gap in a room is a draught. · peel / skin: you peel an apple. · resources / sources: natural resources is the fixed phrase. · issues / facts: problems are issues — environmental issues. · breed / race: a variety of dog is a breed. · food / prey: the animal that is hunted is prey. · the land / the soil / the earth: ground as property or farmland is the land. · stone / pip / nut: the hard seed in a cherry is a stone; a small seed like an apple's is a pip.

One word that fits three sentences

change: climate change, a change of scene, spare change (coins). · occupy: cover an area, keep someone busy, take over a building. · lump: a swelling, a lump in your throat, a lazy lump (a lazy person). · drowned: died in water, (a voice) drowned out by noise, covered a meal in sauce. · straight: a straight line, get something straight (clear), a straight answer (honest).

What you need is not pills but a simple change of scene.
(a change of scene — different surroundings)
Why can't you just give me a straight answer for once in your life?
(a straight answer — an honest, direct one)
Could you peel an apple for me?
(peel — remove the skin of a fruit)

⭐ Key points

  1. Learn collocations as whole phrases: imminent destruction, prophets of doom, a cycle of change, on a global scale.
  2. Weather: draught = cold air in a room, downpour = sudden heavy rain, struck by lightning, thunder = the sound.
  3. Distinguish: prey (hunted animal) ≠ food; breed (animal variety) ≠ race; resources ≠ sources.
  4. Word formation: captive → captivity, survive → survival, danger → endangered, environment → environmentalists, diverse → diversity, hand → handful.
  5. Each animal has its own word set: horse — hoof / saddle / stable; elephant — ivory / trunk / tusks; bat — blind / nocturnal / wing.
  6. One word can fit several sentences with different meanings: change, occupy, lump, drowned, straight — use the context.
