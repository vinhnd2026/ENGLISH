Consolidation 2 — Passive, conditionals and unreal time

This unit has no new grammar — it consolidates four topics: the passive (Grammar 6–7), conditionals (Grammar 8) and unreal time and subjunctives (Grammar 9). The eight exercises below mix the topics together, so you have to spot each pattern from the clues in the sentence — just as in real English use.

a) The passive — forms and special uses

As well as the basic be + past participle, this review focuses on advanced patterns: passives with two objects, have/get something done, reporting structures (it is said that… / he is said to…), and when to leave out the agent (by + …).

b) Conditionals — all three types and their variants

Conditional types 1, 2 and 3 and the mixed conditional; alternatives to if such as unless, supposing and providing; and but for + noun together with if it were not for… for “if it were not for / if it hadn't been for…”.

c) Wish, if only and it's time

Wish / if only + past simple expresses a present wish, and + past perfect a regret about the past; it's (high) time + past simple is used for things that should already be happening.

d) Subjunctives and set patterns

After verbs such as suggest and insist and the pattern be essential that…, use should + infinitive or the bare infinitive; set patterns include be that as it may, suppose… and would rather + past simple with a different subject.

It was revealed last week that production is being transferred to the plant in Luton.
(reporting passive + continuous passive)
The late Prime Minister is said to have been difficult to work with.
(is said to have been — reporting something about the past)
But for Pauline, I would not have passed the exam.
(but for + noun, third conditional)
I wish you had not eaten all the food!
(wish + past perfect — a regret about the past)
It's high time I was starting my homework.
(it's high time + past simple)
Jackson was banned from playing in the next two matches.
(passive, agent omitted)

⭐ Key points

  1. Reporting passives: It is said that… or subject + be said to + infinitive; for an earlier event use to have + past participle (is thought to have been).
  2. Have/get something done: have + object + past participle — arrange for someone to do something, or suffer something happening to your property (Graham had his car towed away).
  3. Omitting the agent: leave out by + agent when the agent is unknown, unimportant or obvious; keep it when it carries new information.
  4. “If it were not for…” conditionals: but for + noun = “if it were not for / if it hadn't been for…”; if it were not for… (present) / if it had not been for… (past).
  5. Wish / if only: + past simple → a present wish; + past perfect → a regret about the past; wish + would → wanting someone to change their behaviour.
  6. It's (high) time + subject + past simple: “it is time to…” (It's time something was done); after suggest / be essential that, use should + infinitive.
