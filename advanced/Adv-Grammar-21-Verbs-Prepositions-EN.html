1. Verbs followed by in, for, of, with

Many English verbs are tied to one fixed preposition — choosing the wrong one makes the sentence incorrect or changes its meaning. The most effective way to learn them is to learn the whole chunk, verb + preposition, as a single unit. This unit organises common verbs by the preposition they take.

a) Verbs + in

be absorbed in · confide in · be engrossed in · implicate someone in · involve someone in · result in · specialise in · succeed in
She specialises in stomach disorders.
(specialise in)
He confided in me about his plans.
(confide in someone)

b) Verbs + for

account for · allow for · apologise for · blame someone for · care for · cater for · charge someone for · count for · earmark something for · pay for
Let me apologise for Jack — he's stuck in traffic.
(apologise for someone = apologise on their behalf)
⚠️ allow for something = take it into consideration, make provision for it; count for = be worth, be valued (typically: count for nothing = be worth nothing).

c) Verbs + of

accuse someone of · convict someone of · remind someone of · suspect someone of
Two men were convicted of fraud.
(convict someone of)
This house reminds me of my own home.
(remind someone of)

d) Verbs + with

acquaint someone with · associate with · charge someone with · coincide with · collide with · comply with · confront someone with · confuse A with B · deal with · discuss something with someone · meet with · plead with · provide someone with · tamper with · trust someone with
The room was cluttered with boxes.
(be cluttered with)
⚠️ Several phrases with with are used almost only in the passive: be cluttered with, be crammed with, be packed with, be faced with. Note also that be concerned with means "be connected with, deal with", quite different from be concerned about = "be worried about" (see section 2d).

2. Verbs followed by from, on, against, about

a) Verbs + from

benefit from · derive something from · deter someone from · differ from · distinguish A from B · distract someone from · exempt someone from · expel someone from · refrain from · resign from · result from · stem from · suffer from · translate something from … into …
His anxiety stemmed from nervous tension and stress.
(stem from)
⚠️ Watch the direction of cause and effect: result in is followed by the result (A causes B), while result from is followed by the cause (B is caused by A). Similarly, we say distinguish A from B or distinguish between A and B.

b) Verbs + on

base something on · blame something on someone · be centred on · concentrate on · decide on · depend on · elaborate on · impose on · insist on · pride oneself on
Alan prides himself on his punctuality.
(pride oneself on)
⚠️ blame has two patterns: blame someone for something (hold a person responsible) and blame something on someone (put the responsibility for a thing on a person).

c) Verbs + against

insure something against · protest against
Is it possible to insure my bike against theft?
(insure something against)

d) Verbs + about

argue about · be concerned about · boast about · decide about · protest about
She is always boasting about her wealthy parents.
(boast about)
⚠️ protest can be used with both against and about: protest against / about the decision.

3. Verbs followed by at, to and other prepositions

a) Verbs + at

glance at · guess at · hint at · marvel at
I marvelled at Jane's ability to jump so high.
(marvel at)

b) Verbs + to

answer to (a description) · appeal to · apply oneself to · attend to · attribute something to · commit oneself to · confess to · devote oneself to · prefer A to B · react to · refer to · refer someone to someone · be resigned to · resort to · see to · subject someone to · succeed to (the throne) · be used to doing
The doctor referred me to a specialist.
(refer someone to someone)
I prefer tea to coffee.
(prefer A to B — never than)
⚠️ be used to + -ing = be accustomed to (not the same as used to + infinitive = did regularly in the past). Answer to a description = match a description (usually of a wanted person). Resort to = turn to something as a last resort.

c) Verbs + out

phase something out
The old system is being phased out.
(phase out)

⭐ Key points

  1. Learn each verb together with its preposition: succeed in, apologise for, accuse of, comply with, refrain from…
  2. Easily confused pairs: accuse someone of but charge someone with; blame someone for something but blame something on someone.
  3. result in + the result (cause something) ↔ result from + the cause (be caused by); stem from also means "be caused by".
  4. Many phrases with with are commonly passive: be packed with, be crammed with, be cluttered with, be faced with.
  5. prefer A to B (never than); be used to + -ing; translate from … into …
  6. Changing the preposition changes the meaning: be concerned about = be worried about ↔ be concerned with = be connected with; protest against / about are both possible.
