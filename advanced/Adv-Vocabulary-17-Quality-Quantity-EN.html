1. Fixed phrases: the quality of life (fixed phrases)

The sailing text is built around fixed phrases worth learning as wholes. To take something for granted is to assume it will always be there and fail to appreciate it. To bring something home to somebody is to make them realise it fully. In my case means “as far as I am concerned / in my situation”. To pull your weight is to do your fair share of the work in a group. At a time gives the amount on each occasion (“a couple of hours at a time”). Soaked to the skin means completely wet, sleeping quarters are the area where people sleep, and to yearn for something is to long for it.

take for granted · bring home to · in my case · pull your weight · at a time · soaked to the skin · sleeping quarters · yearn for
The quality of life these days is something most of us take for granted.
(take for granted — assume something is always there)
It was made clear to me that I'd have to pull my weight.
(pull your weight — do your fair share of the work)
Soon I found myself yearning for my comfortable sheets back home.
(yearn for — long for)

2. Quantity collocations: choosing the word that fits (quantity collocations)

In each pair both words are close in meaning, but only one forms the common collocation: a handful of people (a very small number), a partial success, adequate (enough for what is needed), the bulk of something (the largest part), minute details (extremely small — the adjective is pronounced /maɪˈnjuːt/), a sizeable profit (a fairly large one), a dearth of something (a lack of it), a fair amount of something (quite a lot), limited means (little money available) and a vast difference.

a handful of · partial success · adequate · the bulk of · minute details · a sizeable profit · a dearth of · a fair amount of · limited means · a vast difference
Only a handful of people have shown any interest.
(a handful of — a very small number of)
Sue has already written the bulk of her third novel.
(the bulk of — the largest part of)
There is really a vast difference between the two plans.
(a vast difference — a very large difference)

3. Verbs of increase and decrease; word formation (verbs of increase and decrease; word formation)

Verbs of growing and spreading: extend (make longer, reach further), enlarge (make bigger), augment (increase, add to — formal), spread (reach a wider area). Verbs of shrinking and weakening: reduce (make smaller or lower), decline (gradually become less), contract (become smaller), diminish (become less, weaken), dwindle (gradually disappear almost completely), fade (slowly vanish — used with hope, colour, sound). The word-formation set from the “then and now” text: COMPARE → comparison, YOUNG → youth, ABOUND → abundant, EMPLOY → unemployment, SIZE → sizeable, PURSUE → pursuits, COLLECT → collection, PROSPER → prosperity, TEND → tendency, CRITICISE → critical.

extend · enlarge · augment · spread · reduce · decline · contract · diminish · dwindle · fade
comparison · youth · abundant · unemployment · sizeable · pursuits · collection · prosperity · tendency · critical
Hope has now faded for the two climbers missing since last Friday.
(fade — slowly disappear)
The number of students attending the class dwindled until only two remained.
(dwindle — shrink gradually to almost nothing)
Helen augmented her small salary by making shrewd share dealings.
(augment — increase, add to)

4. Similarity, difference and comments (similarity, difference and comments)

Phrases for sameness and difference: nothing exactly the same as, similar, completely different, are not alike. Two formal replacements: calculated in relation to (for “commensurate with”) and is not as good as we had hoped (for “has fallen short of expectations”). Standard comments used when comparing: There's no comparison (one is clearly far better), It has no equal (nothing matches it), I can't tell the difference, It doesn't come up to expectations, None whatsoever (none at all), It's pointless, You deserve it, It would be less bother, It doesn't count and Personally, I don't think much of it (I don't rate it highly).

nothing exactly the same as · similar · completely different · not alike · calculated in relation to · fall short of expectations · there's no comparison · it has no equal · tell the difference
There is nothing exactly the same as this word in any other language.
(nothing exactly the same as — no exact equivalent)
United are much better than City. There's no comparison.
(there's no comparison — one is clearly far better)
The salary given will be calculated in relation to experience.
(calculated in relation to — worked out according to)

5. Adjectives of quality and quantity; adverbs of degree (adjectives and adverbs of degree)

Adjectives: lavish (grand, extravagant), abundant (plentiful), potential (possible, likely to become — “potential clients”), middling (average, rather poor), excessive (too much), ample (more than enough), superior (better, higher in quality — opposite inferior: lower; “feel inferior” means feel less important), negligible (so small it can be ignored) and major (important, large). Adverbs that replace commoner words: practically = virtually, especially = particularly, altogether = utterly, barely = hardly, thoroughly = completely, respectively = each in that order, moderately = fairly, effectively = to all intents and purposes, considerably = greatly, specifically = clearly and definitely.

lavish · abundant · potential · middling · excessive · ample · superior · negligible · major · inferior
practically · especially · altogether · barely · thoroughly · respectively · moderately · effectively · considerably · specifically
There is no need to rush. We have ample time before the meeting.
(ample — more than enough)
Be careful! I can barely walk!
(barely — only just, almost not)
Classes 3 and 4 scored 10 points and 15 points respectively.
(respectively — each in the order mentioned)

⭐ Key points

  1. Fixed phrases: take for granted, pull your weight, soaked to the skin, yearn for, bring something home to somebody.
  2. Quantity collocations: a handful of, the bulk of, a fair amount of, a dearth of, a vast difference, limited means; minute = extremely small.
  3. Verbs of increase: extend, enlarge, augment, spread; verbs of decrease: reduce, decline, contract, diminish, dwindle, fade.
  4. Similarity and difference: similar, completely different, not alike, nothing exactly the same as; falling short: fall short of expectations = is not as good as we had hoped.
  5. Adjectives: lavish, abundant, ample, excessive, negligible, major, middling, potential; the pair superior ↔ inferior.
  6. Adverbs of degree: practically ≈ virtually, barely ≈ hardly, thoroughly ≈ completely, respectively = in that order, effectively = in practice.
