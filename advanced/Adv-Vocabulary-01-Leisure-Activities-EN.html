1. Equipment for each activity

Every leisure activity has its own set of equipment. A good way to remember the words is to learn them in pairs belonging to one activity, rather than as single words:

a) In the water and outdoors

Scuba diving: flippers · mask  —  Swimming: goggles · armbands
Fishing: rod · bait  —  Walking: rucksack · compass

b) Indoors and with your hands

Gymnastics: horse · bars  —  Photography: tripod · lens
Do-It-Yourself: hammer · spanner  —  Cycling: helmet · pump
While I was rowing across the lake I lost one oar.
(oar — the long pole with a flat end used to row a boat)
During the chess game Carol knocked all the pieces off the board.
(board — the flat surface a game is played on)

2. Match vocabulary

These ten words appear again and again in sports reports and stories — learn them with an example sentence:

board · draw · lap · referee · runner-up · dive · fan · oar · round · whistle
Neither team deserved to lose and the match ended in a draw.
(a draw — a game that ends with equal scores)
Denise won the race and her sister was runner-up.
(runner-up — the person who finishes second)
During the match one of the spectators offered the referee his glasses.
(referee — the official who controls a match)
Ruth was well out in front by the end of the fifth lap.
(lap — one complete circuit of a track or course)

3. Collocations — the fun run

These are the most useful fixed combinations for talking about running and amateur races. Watch the classic pair: take part in means “participate”, while take place means “happen”.

take part in · appeal to me · be cut out for · beneficial effects · raise money for charity · sponsor
gasping for breath · stragglers · cross the finishing line · suffer from exhaustion · personal best · break the course record · on standby · drinks stations · catch up with
Last week well over a thousand people took part in our local fun run.
(take part in — participate in)
The winner broke the course record.
(break a record — do better than the best previous performance)
I go running with my friends regularly now, and I'm actually starting to catch up with them!
(catch up with — reach someone who is ahead of you)

4. Collocations — stress and professional sport

The unit's other two contexts are health (stress) and professional sport. Combinations worth remembering:

a major cause of · factors · point of view · take exercise · stress levels · wind down · a change of scene · thrive on
spectator sports · governing bodies · practices · a branch of the entertainment industry · performers · aspiring champions
It is now generally recognised that stress is a major cause of heart disease.
(a major cause of — one of the main reasons for)
Although there are some individuals who thrive on stress, for most of us, it can lead to exhaustion.
(thrive on — do well in a particular situation)
More recently, sport has become, in effect, a branch of the entertainment industry.
(a branch of — one part of a larger organisation or field)

5. Word formation

The ten base words in this unit form new words with familiar suffixes. Note that equipment is an uncountable noun (it has no plural), and the foreseeable future is a fixed phrase meaning “the near future, as far as we can predict”.

EXPECT → expectations · HAND → handful · REMARK → remarkable · VARY → invariably · EXCEPT → exception
EQUIP → equipment · ACCESS → accessible · DISTINCT → distinguish · INCREASE → increasing · SEE → foreseeable
Helen's solo crossing of the Pacific was a remarkable feat.
(remarkable — unusual and impressive)
Leisure habits won't change much in the foreseeable future.
(the foreseeable future — the near future)
All the runners, with the exception of Mark, were exhausted.
(with the exception of — not including)

6. One word — three sentences

Familiar words often change meaning with the words around them. The five words in this unit are typical examples — learn each combination separately:

a) took — from the verb take

take on (play against / accept) · took (used — took drugs) · take up (start a hobby or activity)

b) board — three different nouns

board of directors · diving board · on the board (chess)

c) low — “low” in several senses

low in confidence · a low turnout (few people came) · a low gear (for going up hills)

d) tread — the verb “step”

tread down (press down by stepping) · tread water (stay upright in water by moving your legs) · tread on (step on)

e) turn — the noun

a turnaround (a complete change, e.g. in a match) · another turn (another go) · a turn of speed (a sudden burst of speed)
On my doctor's advice, I took up yoga in order to relax.
(take up — start doing a new activity)
In any game of chess, the queen is the most powerful piece on the whole board.
(board — the chess board)

⭐ Key points

  1. take part in = participate (≠ take place = happen); take up = start a hobby; take on = play against / accept.
  2. Competition collocations: raise money, break a record, personal best, suffer from exhaustion, be cut out for, catch up with, on standby.
  3. wind down = relax after stress; thrive on = do well in a particular situation; a change of scene = new surroundings to help you relax.
  4. Equipment goes in activity pairs: flippers + mask (diving), rod + bait (fishing), tripod + lens (photography), helmet + pump (cycling).
  5. Word formation: suffixes -tion (expectations, exception), -ful (handful), -able/-ible (remarkable, accessible, foreseeable); equipment is uncountable.
  6. One word, many combinations: board, low, tread, turn change meaning with their partners — learn them in phrases, not alone.
