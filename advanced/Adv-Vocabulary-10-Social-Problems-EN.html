1. Crime and burglary

The opening theme is the fear of being burgled (having your home broken into and robbed): the thief is a burglar, the crime is a burglary, and a house that is robbed is burgled or broken into. Key fixed phrases from this theme:

break into a house · put the record straight · the average burglar · involve · come up with an idea · recover stolen goods · loot / takings

A survey is a study of people's opinions; put the record straight means to correct a false impression; hard-up means short of money; in most cases means usually; loot and takings both mean stolen money and goods; seem is followed by a to-infinitive: don't seem to help.

Eight of them had had their homes broken into more than twice.
(eight of the people surveyed)
To put the record straight, none of my friends owns valuable paintings.
(to put the record straight = to make the truth clear)
The burglars' loot included the family's pet poodle.
(loot = stolen goods)

2. Disasters and emergencies

The ten verbs in the box are all common in reports of disasters — and each one goes with its own fixed partner words:

collapse · evacuate · meet demands · sustain damage · block roads · fail to stop · hold out hope · spread to · fire tear-gas · seal off

Related words: casualties (people killed or injured), hostages (people held prisoner), the blaze (a large fire), uprooted (torn out of the ground), disperse the mob (make a crowd scatter).

The whole building collapsed, but fortunately there were no casualties.
(no casualties = nobody killed or injured)
Rescue teams held out little hope of finding other survivors.
(held out little hope = had almost no hope)
Police sealed off the town-centre for two hours.
(sealed off = blocked entry to)

3. Juvenile crime and drugs — word formation

Adding prefixes and suffixes to change the word class is the core skill in this section:

WORRY → worrying · ADDICT → addiction · OFFENCE → offenders · BEG → beggars · HOME → homeless · SHOP → shoplifting · HARD → hardened · LEGAL → illegally · FORCE → enforce · DETER → deterrent

Phrases to remember: petty crime (minor crime), juvenile crime (crime by young people), a brush with the law (a brief experience of being in trouble with the police), hardened criminals (criminals who will not reform), enforce the law (make people obey the law), a deterrent (something that discourages crime), come to terms with (accept something difficult).

Prison is no deterrent at all for some young people.
(no deterrent = does not discourage them)
It's their job to enforce the law.
(enforce = make people obey)
Kate has had various brushes with the law.
(brushes with the law = minor troubles with the police)

4. Preposition collocations

Many formal phrases are "locked" to one particular preposition — learn the whole phrase:

come into force · on probation · banned from doing sth · an investigation into · on the rampage · out of control · crash into · robbed of · sleep on · charged with

Watch the easily confused pair: rob somebody of something and charge somebody with something; come into force means a law starts to apply; on probation means being supervised after leaving prison.

The new law on dropping litter comes into force next month.
(comes into force = starts to apply)
She was charged with driving without due care and attention.
(charged with = formally accused of)
Several guests at the hotel were robbed of jewellery and money.
(robbed of = had stolen from them)

5. Law, police and the courts

When three similar words are offered, usually only one fits the fixed phrase:

take into custody · the death penalty · help police with their inquiries · a wig · charged with assault · magistrate's court · a first offence · speeding · lack of evidence · the jury's verdict

custody is being held by the police; the death penalty is also called capital punishment; inquiries are police investigations; an offence is the crime itself (a charge is the formal accusation); a verdict is the jury's decision; a case thrown out for insufficient proof is dismissed for lack of evidence.

The police arrested Jack and took him into custody.
(into custody = under police detention)
The court case was dismissed for lack of evidence.
(dismissed = thrown out of court)
'Members of the jury, what is your verdict?'
(verdict = the jury's decision)

⭐ Key points

  1. Burglary phrases: break into a house, be burgled, put the record straight, come up with an idea, in most cases, recover stolen goods, loot / takings.
  2. Disaster verbs: collapse, evacuate, sustain damage, hold out hope, spread (of fire), seal off (an area), meet demands.
  3. Word formation changes word class: addiction (n), offenders (n), homeless (adj), shoplifting (n), hardened (adj), illegally (adv), enforce (v), deterrent (n).
  4. Fixed prepositions: into force · on probation · banned from · investigation into · on the rampage · out of control · crash into · robbed of · sleep on · charged with.
  5. Legal terms: custody, death penalty, inquiries, assault, offence, speeding, evidence, verdict.
  6. Proofreading traps: doubled letters (abandoned, successful, occasions, approval) and punctuation — no comma in known as or indeed the; no apostrophe in the ordinary plural offenders.
