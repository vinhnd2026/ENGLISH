Consolidation 4 — Reported speech, articles, relative clauses and verbs + infinitive or -ing

This unit has no new grammar — it consolidates four areas: reported speech (Grammar 16), articles (Grammar 17), relative and reduced clauses (Grammar 18), and verbs followed by the to-infinitive or -ing (Grammar 19). The seven exercises mix the areas together, so you have to recognise each pattern from the clues in the sentence — just as in real English.

a) Reported speech — choosing the right verb pattern

Each reporting verb has its own pattern: agree, threaten, claim + to-infinitive; warn + object + to-infinitive; deny + -ing; object to + noun. For reports based on what people say, use the passive reporting structure: It is reported that… or subject + be reported to + infinitive.

b) Articles — a/an, the and zero article

Use the for things that are identified or unique, before ordinals (the second half) and superlatives (the best); a/an for one unidentified member of a group; and no article for general meanings, street and town names, and fixed phrases such as for safety's sake and a creature of habit.

c) Relative and reduced clauses

Who for people, which for things, whose for possession, and formal whom, especially after a preposition (for whom); whoever means “anyone who”. Non-defining clauses are separated by commas and never use that; relative clauses can be reduced to -ing phrases or to-infinitives.

d) Verbs + to-infinitive or -ing

Some verbs must be followed by -ing: risk, fancy, spend (time), and remember for memories of past actions. Others take the to-infinitive: manage, train, claim, enable someone to. Try + -ing means experimenting with a method; would rather + bare infinitive expresses a preference.

The President is reported to be in poor health.
(be reported to + infinitive — passive reporting)
Valerie claims to be related to me.
(claim to + infinitive)
That's the last time that I go to a horror film.
(the before last; a before a singular countable noun)
That's the couple whose house my sister bought.
(whose — possession)
Why don't you try phoning Directory Inquiries?
(try + -ing: experiment with a method)
Sue spent a long time looking for the book, without success.
(spend + time + -ing)

⭐ Key points

  1. Passive reporting: be reported / said / thought + to + infinitive (is reported to be); for an earlier event use to have + past participle.
  2. Reporting verbs: agree / threaten / claim + to-infinitive; warn + object + to-infinitive; deny + -ing; object to + noun / -ing.
  3. Articles: the before last, first, best and identified things; a/an for one unidentified member; zero article for general meanings and in phrases like for safety's sake.
  4. Relative words: whom after a preposition in formal style (for whom); whose = of the person/thing; whoever = anyone who, as subject of the clause.
  5. Try / fancy / would rather: try + -ing (experiment with a method); fancy + -ing (feel like doing); would rather + bare infinitive.
  6. -ing and to-infinitive: risk + -ing; spend + time + -ing; manage + to-infinitive; remember + -ing (memory) vs remember + to (not forget to do).
