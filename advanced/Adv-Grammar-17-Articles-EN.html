Advanced · Grammar 17 — Articles

Articles: the with species, groups and place names · Most and the most · A/an and the zero article

1. The definite article — advanced uses

a) Referring to a class or species

The + a singular noun can refer to a whole species or class — a more formal option than using a bare plural.

The tiger is threatened with extinction.
(the species as a whole)

b) National groups and other groups

National groups as a whole take the. A single member of the group is not formed in the same way.

The French eat in restaurants more than the English.
(national groups as a whole)
A Frenchman / a Frenchwoman, an Englishman / an Englishwoman.
(a single person — use a/an)

Groups that are clearly plural also take the: the Social Democrats, The Rolling Stones — but some group names have no article: Pink Floyd, Queen.

c) Unique objects and titles

Objects regarded as unique take the: the moon, the sun. Note, though, that there are other suns and moons in the universe:

This planet has a small moon.
(not the only moon — use a)

Titles tend to be “unique” too: the director of studies. If the title is post-modified (followed by a description), the becomes more likely, though it is not essential:

She became President in 1998. — She became (the) President of the United States in 1998.
(a post-modified title makes the more likely)

d) The as part of a name

Sometimes the is part of the name itself and is capitalised, for example in newspaper names: The Independent, The Sunday Times.

e) Musical instruments

After verbs for playing an instrument, use the. But where a would naturally be used, it is still possible:

Jane plays the flute.
(musical instruments — use the)
There was a small brown flute in the window of the shop.
(one particular flute, not specified — use a)

f) Emphatic use

The can be heavily stressed to emphasise the noun that follows — “the one and only”:

This hotel is the place to stay.
(the is heavily stressed)

2. The with geographical and place names

a) Names that take the

rivers: the Thames · mountain ranges: the Alps · oceans and seas: the Mediterranean · unique features: the Channel, the Arctic · compass points / areas: the East, the Middle East · collective or plural countries: The United Kingdom, The Netherlands

The is also sometimes used before Lebanon and Gambia: the Lebanon, the Gambia.

b) Names that do not take the

mountain peaks: Everest (but: The Matterhorn) · continents: Asia · ordinary countries: France

c) Place names and “of” phrases

Post-modification, especially with … of …, plays a role in place names. Compare:

Leeds University / The University of Leeds — London Bridge / The Tower of London.
(names with an of phrase usually take the)

If the first part of a place name is itself another name, the normal zero-article rules apply: Brown's Restaurant (but The Garden House Hotel). The same applies in geographical names: Canvey Island but The Isle of Man.

3. Most and the most — and the importance of context

a) Most (no the) — a generalisation

Most hotels in England are very expensive.
(making a generalisation)

b) The most — a specific superlative

This is the most expensive hotel in town.
(talking about one specific hotel)

c) The depends on context

The refers to items already mentioned, or already clear, so its use depends on the context:

The Smiths had a son and a daughter. The son was in the Army and the daughter was training to be a doctor.
(the — because they have already been mentioned)

In the same way, the Saturday here refers to a day in a period of time that has already been mentioned (“on the Saturday of that week”):

On the Saturday, there was a terrible storm.
(the Saturday of that week)

4. The indefinite article (a/an) and the zero article

a) A/an — jobs and roles

Tony is a builder. — Tony was the builder of that house.
(a job — a; one specific person — the)

b) A/an — in measuring

A/an can mean “per” in measurements. Formally, per can replace it:

three times a week · fifty kilometres an hour · £3.50 a kilo · £15,000 a year

c) A/an — unknown people

Using a/an before a person's name emphasises that the person is unknown to the speaker:

A Mr Jones called while you were out.
(a person the speaker does not know)

d) Zero article — people and their works

A person's name on its own refers to the person; add a and it refers to a work by that person:

Matthew Smith is one of my favourite artists. — A Matthew Smith hangs in their bedroom.
(the person — a painting by him)

e) Zero article — organisations and streets

Some unique organisations do not use the: Parliament (but The (House of) Commons). Most streets have no article either: Green Road, Godwin Street. Exceptions are The High Street, The Strand, and street names without a preceding adjective: compare Holly Drive with The Drive.

⚠️ The same noun may or may not take an article depending on the meaning — when translating into another language, judge by the meaning, not by the form. Examples: I know how to use a computer. A pound and a half of cheese. Terry has flu. I've got a headache.

⭐ Key points

  1. The + a singular noun can refer to a whole class or species (the tiger) — more formal than a bare plural.
  2. National groups as a whole take the (the French), but a single person takes a/an (a Frenchman).
  3. Geographical names: rivers, mountain ranges, seas, areas and collective country names take the (the Thames, the Alps, The Netherlands); peaks, continents and ordinary countries do not (Everest, Asia, France).
  4. Place names with an of phrase usually take the: The University of Leeds, The Tower of London.
  5. Most = a generalisation about the majority; the most = the superlative of one specific thing.
  6. The is used for things already mentioned or clear from the context (the son…, on the Saturday).
  7. A/an is used for jobs, measurements (three times a week) and unknown people (a Mr Jones); street names and some organisations take the zero article (Parliament, Green Road — exceptions: The High Street).
