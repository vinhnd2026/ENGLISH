1. Inspections and school reports

When a school becomes notorious, government inspectors visit it, and their report usually highlights the most serious problems, such as poor standards of teaching. Reporters enter the school by prior arrangement (by an agreement made beforehand) and witness the chaos at first hand (directly, with their own eyes). A disruptive pupil may be given a suspension (temporary exclusion) — not to be confused with expulsion, being thrown out of the school for good. Pupils who run riot behave in an uncontrolled, wild way, and to abuse someone here means to insult them verbally. In a school report, teachers write positive comments such as make a big effort, mature for her age, apply herself (work hard and concentrate), contribute to class discussions and show respect; negative ones include insolent (rude and disrespectful), half-hearted (without enthusiasm), easily distracted, finding it hard to concentrate, and not participating in group work.

inspectors · highlight · standards · prior arrangement · at first hand · suspension · run riot · abuse · effort · mature · apply oneself · contribute · respect · insolent · half-hearted · distracted · concentrate · participate
The report highlighted poor standards of teaching.
The report drew attention to how badly the school was taught.
One disruptive pupil was given a three-week suspension for punching a teacher in the face.
A three-week exclusion was the punishment for hitting a teacher.
Tracey applies herself well and contributes fully to class discussions.
Tracey works hard and takes an active part in lessons.

2. Education collocations

Many education expressions are fixed collocations where only one of two near-synonyms sounds natural: core subjects (the main compulsory subjects), academic achievement, corporal punishment (physical punishment such as hitting), a support teacher (who helps pupils with learning difficulties), a trainee teacher, continuous assessment (marks built up over the course rather than in one final exam), vocation-oriented courses, a work placement (a period of work experience), teenage mothers, and play truant (stay away from school without permission — note play, not go).

core subjects · academic achievement · corporal punishment · support teacher · trainee teacher · continuous assessment · vocation-oriented · work placement · teenage mothers · play truant
In my country, corporal punishment was abolished 40 years ago.
Physical punishment of pupils has been illegal for four decades.
At 16, we have the choice of doing more vocation-oriented courses, such as business studies and accounting.
Older students can choose courses aimed at a job.
I was expelled from school for playing truant too many times.
He was thrown out for repeatedly staying away from school.

3. Word formation: familiar roots and suffixes

The word-formation exercise recycles very common patterns: PROBLEM → problematic (adjective, -atic), INVOLVE → involvement (noun, -ment), BUREAU → bureaucratic, ENCHANT → disenchanted (the prefix dis- reverses the meaning: no longer charmed by something), AGGRESSIVE → aggression, RUDE → rudeness (-ness), POWER → powerless (-less = without), BORING → boredom (-dom), REDUCE → reduction, RESPECT → respectful (-ful). When you do the exercise, predict the word class from the gap first: a noun follows an adjective, an adjective follows more, and an adjective comes before a noun.

problematic · involvement · bureaucratic · disenchanted · aggression · rudeness · powerless · boredom · reduction · respectful
The job is now ten times more bureaucratic than it was when I started out.
There is far more paperwork and procedure than before.
Standard national tests have left many teachers disenchanted with their profession.
Many teachers have lost their enthusiasm for teaching.
Teachers have become almost powerless to stop the frequent little acts of rudeness.
Teachers can do almost nothing about everyday bad behaviour.

4. Learning experiences, and one word with several meanings

The five speakers use everyday education language: a graduation ceremony, a degree, a student loan to pay back, and a scholarship; being sent out of the lesson or put in detention (kept at school as a punishment), extra lessons with a private tutor, and getting the top mark; leaving school without any qualifications and with no real job prospects, then taking evening classes at a further education college; having to revise for exams, getting disappointing grades and having to retake / resit them, but winning a prize at Sports Day; and becoming a mature student, signing up for a correspondence course ('distance learning'), sending assignments to a tutor and choosing to specialise in one area. Finally, a single word can fit three sentences with three different meanings: mark (a grade / an impression / a stain), applies (is true of / asks for a job / works hard at), dropped (gave up / left college / withdrew charges), ruled (governed strictly / decided in court / ruled out = excluded a possibility) and support (in support of, moral support, a support teacher).

graduation ceremony · degree · loan · scholarship · sent out · detention · private tutor · top mark · qualifications · prospects · evening classes · further education · revise · grades · retake · prize · mature student · correspondence course · assignments · specialise
She has a scholarship to study at Yale University.
Her studies at Yale are paid for by an award.
I left school without any qualifications, and with no real job prospects.
He had no certificates and little chance of a good job.
The entire workforce downed tools today, in support of two sacked colleagues.
All the workers went on strike to back two dismissed colleagues.

⭐ Key points

  1. Inspectors visit and assess schools; a report highlights problems and comments on standards of teaching.
  2. suspension = temporary exclusion; expulsion = being thrown out for good; run riot = behave wildly; by prior arrangement = agreed beforehand; at first hand = seen directly.
  3. Good report comments: make a big effort, mature, apply oneself, contribute, show respect; bad ones: insolent, half-hearted, distracted.
  4. Fixed collocations: core subjects, academic achievement, corporal punishment, support teacher, trainee teacher, continuous assessment, work placement, play truant (not go truant).
  5. Word formation: INVOLVE → involvement, ENCHANT → disenchanted, POWER → powerless, BORING → boredom, REDUCE → reduction, RESPECT → respectful.
  6. One word, several meanings: mark, applies (apply for a job), dropped (drop out of college), ruled (rule out = exclude), support (in support of, moral support).
