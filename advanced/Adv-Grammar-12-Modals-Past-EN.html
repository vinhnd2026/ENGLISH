Advanced · Grammar 12 — Modals: past

Past modals: had to · should have · could have · may / might have · must have / can't have · would have · needn't have

1. Past obligation and should have

a) had to — the past form of must (past obligation)

S + had to + V  ·  negative: didn't have to + V

Must has no past form, so a past obligation is expressed with had to. The negative didn't have to means there was no obligation — “it wasn't necessary”. (Note: must have + past participle expresses certainty, not obligation — see card 4.)

Sorry I'm late, I had to post some letters.
(a past obligation)

2. should have / ought to have — expectations and criticism

S + should have + V3  ·  S + ought to have + V3 (ought to can replace should in most of these uses)

a) Expectation — something was supposed to happen

Should have + V3 talks about something that was expected to have happened already.

The parcel I sent you should have arrived by now.
(expected by now)

b) Criticism of a past action

You shouldn't have eaten so much last night.
(criticising what the person did)

c) After verbs of thinking, and with be + adjectives of chance

After verbs like think and expect, should have matches an unreal past form in the clause that follows. With be and adjectives describing chance (strange, odd…) it means “it was strange that…”.

I should have thought you knew.
(= I would have expected you to know)
It was strange that you should have been staying in the same hotel last year.
(a strange coincidence)

d) A polite way of saying thank you

When you receive a gift or a favour, You shouldn't have! is a friendly thank-you — not a criticism.

I've done the washing up for you. — Oh, you really shouldn't have!
(a friendly thank-you)

3. Past possibility: could, could have, may/might have

a) could — past permission or ability (things that actually happened)

S + could + V

Could talks about general permission or ability in the past — the action really happened. Compare could have + V3 in b): something that was possible but did not happen.

When I was sixteen I could stay out till 11.00.
(permission)
Mary could swim when she was three.
(she really did swim)
Mary could have swum when she was three.
(but she didn't)

b) could have — unfulfilled possibility, uncertainty, unwillingness

S + could have + V3  ·  S + couldn't have + V3

Could have + V3 expresses a past possibility or ability that was not used, or an uncertain guess about the past. Couldn't have works for both meanings too, and is common with comparative adjectives. Could have can also show unwillingness: something was possible, but the person chose not to do it.

David could have won the race if he had tried.
(possibility — but he didn't win)
It could have been Sue, I suppose.
(uncertainty)
We couldn't have been happier in those days.
(with a comparative adjective)
She could have gone to the party with her friends, but she didn't.
(unwillingness)
We couldn't have left the dog on its own, so we didn't.
(we weren't willing to)

c) may have / might have — near misses, blame, and an idiom

S + may have + V3  ·  S + might have + V3

Might have + V3 can describe something that nearly happened but didn't. Both may have and might have express uncertainty about the past, in positive and negative sentences. With strong stress, might have blames someone for not doing something. The idiom I might have known (that)… says, ironically, that someone's behaviour was completely typical of them.

You might have drowned!
(it nearly happened — luckily it didn't)
I suppose I may have been rather critical.
(uncertainty)
They might not have received our letter yet.
(negative uncertainty)
You might have told me my trousers were split!
(blaming someone)
I might have known that he would be late.
(the idiom: “typical!”)

4. Certainty, would, and unnecessary actions

a) must have / can't have — certainty about the past

S + must have + V3 (certain it happened)  ·  S + can't have + V3 (certain it didn't happen)

Both express the speaker's certainty about a past action, and both are common with surely in exclamations.

Someone must have taken it.
(I am sure they did)
You can't have lost it.
(I am sure you didn't)
Surely you can't have eaten all of it!
(with surely)
Surely you must have noticed it!
(with surely)

b) wouldn't — past unwillingness; would have — unreal events and assumptions

S + wouldn't + V (past refusal)  ·  S + would have + V3

Would not (wouldn't) expresses refusal or unwillingness in the past. Would have + V3 refers to past events that did not actually happen, and is also used for assumptions about the past.

Everyone was angry because Sam wouldn't turn off the television.
(refusal)
I would have accepted this job, but I didn't want to move house.
(an event that didn't happen)
A: Someone called after you left but didn't leave a message. B: That would have been Cathy, probably.
(an assumption about the past)

c) needn't have and didn't need to — unnecessary actions

needn't have + V3 = it was done but unnecessary  ·  didn't need to + V = it wasn't necessary, and it wasn't done

Both talk about a lack of necessity in the past; the difference is whether the action was actually carried out.

You needn't have paid all at once.
(you did pay — but it wasn't necessary)
I didn't need to go to the dentist again, luckily.
(and I didn't go)

d) Adverbs that emphasise modals: well, easily, obviously, really, just

Adverbs such as well, easily, obviously, really and just are often used to emphasise modal expressions, in both present and past time.

You could easily have been killed.
(easily = very possibly)
She obviously must have left.
(obviously = clearly)
You couldn't really have managed without me.
(really = truly)
I might well decide to come.
(a present-time example: well = quite probably)

⭐ Key points

  1. had to is the past form of must (obligation); didn't have to = it wasn't necessary. Don't confuse it with must have + V3 (certainty).
  2. should have / ought to have + V3: something was expected to happen; criticism of a past action; and the polite You shouldn't have! when thanking someone.
  3. could + V = past permission/ability (it really happened); could have + V3 = it was possible but didn't happen, or an uncertain guess.
  4. may have / might have + V3: past possibility and uncertainty; You might have told me! = blame; I might have known = “typical!”
  5. must have + V3 = certainly happened; can't have + V3 = certainly didn't happen.
  6. wouldn't = past refusal; would have + V3 = something that didn't happen, or an assumption about the past.
  7. needn't have + V3 = done but unnecessary; didn't need to + V = not necessary and not done.
  8. Adverbs well, easily, obviously, really, just emphasise modals: could easily have been killed, might well have…
