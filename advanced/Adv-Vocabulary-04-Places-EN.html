1. Homes and neighbourhoods

This unit focuses on vocabulary for describing homes: types of accommodation, the condition of a property and the area around it.

a) Words for a place to live

residence · accommodation · premises · habitation

Residence is a formal word for a home. Accommodation is the general, uncountable word for a place to live or stay. Premises refers to a building and its land, especially in legal language. Habitation is rare and means the act of living in a place.

When I first arrived here to take up my new job, I stayed in a hotel, but I soon started looking for a permanent residence.
(residence — a formal word for a home)

b) The surrounding area

neighbourhood · surroundings · vicinity

A neighbourhood is the area around your home. Surroundings is the general word for everything around a place. Vicinity is more formal, usually in the phrase in the vicinity of.

The house was in a quiet neighbourhood, and as soon as I saw it I fell in love with it.
(neighbourhood — the local area)

c) Condition and facilities

furnished · sparsely furnished · patches of damp · storage space · parking space

A furnished home already has furniture in it; fully furnished means completely equipped. Patches of damp are areas of moisture on the walls. Storage space is room for keeping things, and a parking space is a place to park a car.

The first flat I came across was cold and uninviting, and had large patches of damp on the walls.
(come across — find by chance; patches of damp)
There was, however, quite a lot of storage space.
(storage space — room for keeping things)

d) Renting and details around the house

let · rent · hedge · drive · bay window

To let a room is to rent it out (a room to let); the person who pays to live there rents it. A hedge is a fence made of bushes; a drive is the short road from the street to the house; a bay window curves out from a wall.

The room to let looked out over the back garden, and had a big bay window.
(a room to let; look out over; a bay window)
There was a high overgrown hedge around the front garden, and room to park cars in the drive.
(a hedge; room to park — space for parking)

2. One word — several meanings

Some English words have very different meanings; the context decides which one is meant. Each of these five words works in three quite different situations.

a) summit — a mountain top / the highest point / a meeting of leaders

It took us three hours of hard climbing to reach the summit.
(the top of a mountain)
Being promoted to manager is undoubtedly the summit of my career to date.
(the highest point of a career)
A special summit of the leading economic countries has been called.
(a meeting of government leaders)

b) key — a door key / an answer key / the decisive factor

When the agreement is finally signed by all parties, you will receive the key to the house.
(the key to a house)
The self-study edition of the book comes with a key so you can check all your answers.
(an answer key)
His ability to persuade people is the key to his success.
(the decisive factor)

c) state — condition / state of mind / head of state

The second flat I saw was in a terrible state.
(the condition of something)
His physical condition is improving, but I'm not sure about his state of mind.
(state of mind — mental condition)
The funeral of President Mawaka was attended by heads of state from all over the world.
(heads of state — leaders of countries)

d) view — scenery / intention / opinion

The A23 out of the city affords a spectacular view of the lake.
(a view over the lake)
I would like to look at the house again, with a view to moving in shortly.
(with a view to + -ing — with the intention of)
You won't find a better house than that one; that's my view anyway.
(an opinion)

e) press — move on / iron / push

OK, let's press on and try to reach the top by lunchtime.
(press on — continue moving forward)
These trousers are specially designed so that you don't need to iron and press them.
(press clothes — iron them)
If you press this button here, you'll activate the alarm system.
(press a button)

3. Landscape features

Ten words for features of the natural landscape:

spring · horizon · tide · cliff · strait · pass · bay · slope · landscape · plain
  • spring — water that flows out of the ground
  • horizon — the line where the land meets the sky
  • tide — the rise and fall of the sea; the tide comes in means the sea is rising
  • cliff — a steep rock face, usually by the sea
  • strait — a narrow channel of sea between two areas of land
  • pass — a route through mountains
  • bay — a curved area of sea enclosed by land
  • slope — a surface that slopes, e.g. the side of a hill
  • landscape — the scenery of an area
  • plain — a large flat area of land
Many small boats could be seen moored in the wide curving bay.
(a bay)
The two islands are divided by a narrow strait.
(a strait)
At the foot of the mountains was a wide, well-cultivated plain.
(a plain)

4. Parts of a house

landing · tiles · railings · mantelpiece · porch · doormat · slates · radiator · hinges · loft
  • landing — the flat area at the top of a flight of stairs
  • tiles — flat pieces used to cover walls or floors
  • railings — a fence made of metal bars
  • mantelpiece — the shelf above a fireplace
  • porch — a covered area in front of the entrance to a house
  • doormat — a mat for wiping your feet on
  • slates — thin pieces of stone used to cover a roof
  • radiator — a heater that fills with hot water
  • hinges — the metal joints that allow a door to open
  • loft — the space under the roof of a house
After the storm we had to replace several slates which had fallen off the roof.
(slates on a roof)
We stored our old books upstairs in the loft, in case we needed them again.
(a loft)

5. Collocations and the language of agreements

a) Common collocations

sparsely furnished · tenancy agreement · household rubbish · squatters' rights · private property · ten-storey building · side entrance · sharp rise · barely adequate · sheer drop

Some adjectives and nouns combine in fixed ways: a room with very little furniture is sparsely furnished; rubbish from homes is household rubbish; a big, fast increase is a sharp rise; a vertical cliff face is a sheer drop; and something that is only just enough is barely adequate.

There's been a sharp rise in the price of property in the south-east.
(a sharp rise)
From the cliff top, it was a sheer drop to the rocks below.
(a sheer drop)

b) Formal words in a tenancy agreement

Legal documents use formal words instead of everyday ones:

forbidden (= not allowed) · occupants · maintain in good condition · in the event of (= if there is) · notify / inform · observe the rules (= keep to) · be evicted (= be made to leave) · in advance · vacate the premises (= leave) · access · inspect · prospective tenants · view a property · be removed
Please inform us 28 days in advance of your intention to vacate the premises.
(in advance; vacate the premises)
Persistent failure to observe the above rules may result in you, the tenant, being evicted.
(observe the rules; be evicted)

⭐ Key points

  1. residence (formal) = a home; accommodation = a place to live in general; neighbourhood = the local area; vicinity = the area near a place.
  2. let = rent out, rent = pay to live somewhere; furnished = with furniture, sparsely furnished = with very little furniture.
  3. One word, several meanings: summit (mountain top / highest point / leaders' meeting), key (door key / answer key / decisive factor), state (condition / head of state), view (scenery / opinion), press (move on / iron / push).
  4. Landscape: cliff, strait, pass, bay, plain, slope.
  5. In the house: landing, mantelpiece, loft, slates, hinges.
  6. Agreement language: in the event of = if there is; vacate the premises = leave; be evicted = be forced to leave; prospective tenants = possible future tenants.
