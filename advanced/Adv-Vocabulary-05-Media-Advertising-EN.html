1. Print and publishing

The first group of words is about books, newspapers and things we read in print.

a) Books and manuscripts

instruction manual · novel · edition · fiction · manuscript · prose · literature · outline · gist · shorthand

instruction manual — the book of instructions that comes with a machine or device.
novel — a long written story; an autobiographical novel is based on the writer's own life.
edition — a particular version of a book published at one time; a first edition of a rare book can be worth a fortune.
fiction — stories that are invented, not true (the opposite of fact).
manuscript — the author's original text of a book before it is printed.
prose — ordinary written language (the opposite of poetry).
literature — written works, especially valued ones; to study French literature at university.
outline — a summary of the main points of a story or article.
gist — the main idea: to get the gist of the article is to understand its main point without reading every detail.
shorthand — a fast system of abbreviated writing used to take down speech.

b) Reading and writing

illegible — (handwriting) impossible to read because it is so untidy.
illiterate — unable to read or write.
unprintable — too rude or offensive to be printed.
out of print — (of a book) no longer being printed or sold.

'David Copperfield' is an autobiographical novel.
A first edition of this book is worth a fortune.
Sheila left the manuscript of her novel on a train by mistake.
I understood the gist of the article, but I didn't read it in detail.

2. TV, broadcasting and news

a) Programmes and bulletins

weather forecast — a report in advance of what the weather will be.
party political broadcast — a television or radio programme in which a political party presents its views.
news bulletin — a short news report giving the main points.
coverage — the reporting of a particular event: sports coverage is everything a channel shows and reports about sport. Reporting is the general activity; coverage is the complete reporting of one event or subject.
mass media — newspapers, radio and television considered together.
correspondent — a journalist who specialises in one subject or reports from one place: a political correspondent. Journalist is the general word.
circulation — the number of copies of a newspaper or magazine sold.

What time is the next news bulletin?
This channel doesn't have very good sports coverage.
This report comes from our political correspondent, Edward Ross.

b) Talking about television and its audience

be glued to the screen — to watch without looking away.
stare blankly — to look for a long time without expression or understanding.
dumbing down — making programmes simpler and less intellectual to appeal to more people.
sensationalise — to exaggerate news to make it seem more exciting or shocking.
outnumber — to be greater in number.
howls of protest — loud, angry protests.
serve a purpose — to be useful for a purpose; serve no useful purpose = be completely useless.
take a stand against — to state your opposition to something publicly.

He sits there for hour after hour, staring blankly at the screen.

3. Advertising

advertising campaign — a planned series of advertisements.
brochure — a small book or leaflet advertising something; a holiday brochure describes holidays you can book.
target customer — the customer an advertisement aims at (not intended customer, which sounds unnatural).
project an image — to create and present an image of a product or company.
subliminal messages — hidden signals that affect people without them being aware of it.
censorship — the banning or removal of material that is considered harmful or offensive.

When does our new advertising campaign begin?
I spent all of yesterday evening looking at this holiday brochure.
Through market research the advertising company identified their target customer.
They are very concerned with the image that the advert projects.

4. Word formation and spelling

a) Word formation

spoon → spoonful · head → headlong · terrify → terrifying · controversy → controversial · accept → unacceptable · imply → implication · interpret → interpretation · intention → intended · conscious → subconscious · encourage → encouragement

spoonful — as much as a spoon holds (the suffix -ful means the amount a container holds).
headlong — rushing forwards with the head first; in a hurried, reckless way.
terrifying — extremely frightening.
controversial — causing public argument and disagreement.
unacceptable — impossible to accept (the negative prefix un-).
implication — something suggested without being said directly.
interpretation — a way of understanding or explaining something.
intended — planned or meant on purpose.
subconscious — belonging to the part of the mind we are not aware of (the prefix sub- means “under”).
encouragement — words or actions that give someone confidence or hope.

b) Spelling traps

⚠️ Words that are easy to misspell: dissatisfaction (double s), embarrassing (rr and ss), phenomenon (begins with ph-, singular ends in -on), extraordinary, assumptions and permission (ss), benefited (only one t), personalities, concerned. Apostrophes are also often added wrongly: ordinary plurals are written copies, moments — with no apostrophe.
A man takes a single spoonful of a substance and puts it in his mouth.
He rushes headlong into this parallel universe.
I was told that it didn't contain any subconscious messages.

⭐ Key points

  1. Fixed media collocations: instruction manual, weather forecast, news bulletin, party political broadcast, first edition, mass media, advertising campaign, holiday brochure.
  2. Distinctions: coverage (the reporting of one event) vs reporting in general; correspondent (a specialist or resident reporter) vs journalist in general; circulation = the number of copies a newspaper sells; out of print = no longer printed.
  3. Reading words: gist (main idea), outline (summary), manuscript (author's original), prose, shorthand, illegible (unreadable), illiterate (unable to read), unprintable (too rude to print).
  4. Television phrases: be glued to the screen, dumbing down, sensationalise the news, howls of protest, serve no useful purpose, take a stand against.
  5. Advertising: target customer, project an image, subliminal messages, censorship.
  6. Word formation: -ful (spoonful), un- (unacceptable), sub- (subconscious), -tion (implication, interpretation), -ment (encouragement); watch the spelling of dissatisfaction, embarrassing, phenomenon.
