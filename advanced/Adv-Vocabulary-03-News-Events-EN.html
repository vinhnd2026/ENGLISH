1. Reading the news: core phrases from a news report

This unit is about news language: how British newspapers report events. The first group of items comes from a report about plans for a new runway — notice how the verbs come in fixed partnerships (collocations):

give the go-ahead (to) · local residents · controversial · involve · as well as · volume of traffic · the foreseeable future · be regarded as · prospect · suffer from · be made up of · go back on a promise · have no intention of · stand by a pledge · be quoted as saying
Reports that the government is about to give the go-ahead to plans for a new runway have angered local residents.
(give the go-ahead = officially allow something to happen)
We believe that the government has a duty to stand by its pledges.
(stand by = keep, not break, a promise)

2. News collocations — when both options “make sense”

In many items you choose between two similar words; the right answer is the one that forms the standard partnership (collocation) in news style:

disguised as · sole survivor · extensively damaged · critical condition · comment on · detained for questioning · the findings of a committee · tackle a problem · sensitive issues · baffled by

Transformed also means “changed”, but people who dress up as guards are disguised as them; and while results is a general word, the official conclusions of a committee are its findings.

The pilot was the sole survivor of the crash.
(sole = only)
Detectives were baffled by Mr Day's disappearance.
(baffled = completely puzzled)

3. Sports news: the ten box words and their phrases

The sports items recycle familiar abstract nouns, but always inside fixed phrases:

little prospect of · conditions · common knowledge · an ugly incident · secure her place · on the verge of · a matter of opinion · have every confidence · concrete evidence · disclaim responsibility
With Smith out injured, there is little prospect of City reaching the next round.
(little prospect of = very small chance of)
The final day begins with the Australian team on the verge of victory.
(on the verge of = very close to)

4. Fixed phrases in negotiation and industrial news

a) Endings that complete the phrase

draw up proposals · put a strain on · hold out much hope · pride oneself on · on a regular basis · challenge the figures · jump at the chance · moderate a demand · out of all proportion · at the disposal of

b) Phrases used for rewriting (more formal equivalents)

have no intention of · it is common knowledge that · bring about (= cause) · raise fears · little prospect of success · say for certain · argue that there should be · explain the cause as
No one holds out much hope for the success of the discussions.
(hold out hope = feel hopeful — usually negative)
The company prided itself on its good relations with all its employees.
(pride oneself on = be proud of)

5. Headline language

Headlines must be short and punchy: articles and auxiliary verbs are dropped, the present simple is used for recent events, and short words replace whole phrases:

bid = attempt · clash = disagree / fight · held = arrested · toll = number of people killed · boost = increase · cleared = found innocent · looms = approaches (something bad) · set = ready / about to · vows = promises
Ministers clash over pay rises.
(a full sentence would be: Ministers are in disagreement over pay rises)
Rail strike looms.
(looms is used for something unpleasant that is coming closer)

6. Word formation in a news text

The report about a possible general election is full of abstract nouns built from verbs and adjectives. Learn them as word families:

SPECULATE → speculation · ANNOUNCE → announcement · ANALYSE → analysts · SURVIVE → survival · ASSURE → assurances · THINK → unthinkable · POLITICS → political · DOWN → downfall · CRITICISE → criticism · DISASTER → disastrous · FAIL → failure · EMPLOY → unemployment · UNITE → unity · DIVIDE → divisions · JUSTIFY → justification
Press speculation continues over whether the Prime Minister is on the point of calling a General Election.
(speculation = guessing about what may happen)
An announcement is expected shortly from government headquarters.
(announcement = an official statement)

⭐ Key points

  1. Collocation is the key to news language: give the go-ahead, stand by a pledge, go back on a promise, suffer from, be made up of.
  2. When both options make sense, choose the word that forms the fixed partnership with its neighbour: sole survivor, critical condition, comment on, tackle a problem.
  3. Frequent sports phrases: on the verge of, a matter of opinion, common knowledge, disclaim responsibility, little prospect of.
  4. Fixed phrases in negotiations: draw up proposals, hold out much hope, jump at the chance, out of all proportion, at the disposal of.
  5. Headlines use short words instead of long phrases (bid, clash, toll, boost, looms, vows) and drop articles and auxiliaries.
  6. Word formation: master the noun suffixes -tion, -ment, -al, -ure and the negative prefix un- (unthinkable, unemployment).
