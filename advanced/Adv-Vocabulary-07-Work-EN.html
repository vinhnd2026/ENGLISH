1. Collocations for getting a job

In English, many nouns combine with one fixed partner word, and learning the whole partnership is the surest way to sound natural. The ten pairs in this unit follow the whole path into a new job: you send a covering letter with your CV, choose a career path with good promotion prospects, send in a claims form so your travel expenses are repaid, check the working conditions and receive your job description, then complete a trial period before you qualify for sick pay and can join the company pension scheme.

covering letter · career path · promotion prospects · claims form · travel expenses · working conditions · job description · trial period · sick pay · pension scheme
She sent in her CV and a covering letter.
(a letter sent together with a CV)
After a trial period of one month, I'll be on a permanent contract with sick pay.
(pay you receive when you are ill and cannot work)

2. People at work: titles and roles

Each word below names a different position or role in the world of work — take care not to confuse them:

· executive: a senior manager with decision-making power in a company.
· foreman: the worker in charge of a group of workers — the person to talk to if problems arise.
· producer: the company or person that makes a product.
· trainee: someone who is being trained for the job.
· agent: a person or company acting on behalf of another company.
· industrialist: a wealthy owner of manufacturing businesses.
· competitor: a rival company.
· labourer: a worker doing unskilled physical work.
· dealer: someone who buys and sells a particular product, such as used cars.
· client: a customer of a professional service.

If you have any problems with your work, talk to the foreman.
(the person in charge of a group of workers)
A company should make every client feel important.
(client = a customer of a service)

3. Word formation and the formal language of contracts

a) Word formation

Suffixes change the word class: PRODUCT → productivity, INVEST → investments, QUALIFY → qualifications, EXPERT → expertise, ACT → action (note industrial action — action such as a strike, taken by workers), REPRESENT → representatives, ECONOMY → economise (to cut spending), CONSULT → consultants, STREAM → streamlined, CLEAR → clarified.

b) Words in letters and contracts

Contract language is more formal than everyday speech: eligible (allowed to join or receive something), entitled to (having the right to), negotiable (open to discussion), smartly dressed, commence (a formal word for start), overtime (extra hours and the pay for them), absence, a doctor's certificate, a contract that is terminated (ended), and the advance warning of leaving, called notice.

Union representatives eventually sat down with management and negotiated a 4 per cent pay rise.
(representatives = people chosen to speak for a group)
You are entitled to 25 days' holiday. The salary is not negotiable.
(entitled to = having the right to; negotiable = open to discussion)

4. Idioms about work

· be headhunted: to be approached by another company and offered a better job.
· at the end of your tether: having run out of patience.
· be given the sack: to be dismissed from a job.
· have your nose to the grindstone: to work hard without a break.
· a golden handshake: a large sum of money given when someone leaves a company.
· take on (a new employee): to employ someone.
· be on the go: to be busy all the time.
· be overlooked: not to be considered, for example for promotion.
· make good: to become successful.
· keep someone on their toes: to make someone stay alert and never relax.

Mary's assistant was given the sack.
(was dismissed)
Pauline's boss keeps her on her toes.
(never lets her relax or become careless)

⭐ Key points

  1. Learn collocations as whole pairs: covering letter, career path, promotion prospects, claims form, travel expenses, working conditions, job description, trial period, sick pay, pension scheme.
  2. People at work: executive (senior manager) · foreman (in charge of workers) · trainee (being trained) · labourer (unskilled worker) · agent (acts for another company) · dealer (buys and sells) · client (customer) · producer (maker) · industrialist (business owner) · competitor (rival).
  3. Word formation: suffixes change the word class — productivity, qualifications, expertise, representatives, consultants; note industrial action (a strike) and economise (cut spending).
  4. Contract language is formal: eligible, entitled to, negotiable, commence, terminated, notice replace everyday words.
  5. Work idioms: get the sack (be dismissed) is the opposite outcome of a golden handshake (money on leaving); be overlooked = not considered for promotion; make good = become successful.
  6. Watch for fixed phrases in formal letters: in respect of, at any time, in writing, one month's notice.
