1. Interpreting other people's feelings

To understand what others feel, we rely on more than the actual words they say: facial expression and tone of voice show our reaction to something, and may express views we are trying to hide. A tactful person picks up these signals. Interviewers pay particular attention to how a job candidate walks in and sits down, because they want to know about the candidate's character traits and psychological stability. Making candidates take psychological tests can be seen as an intrusion into their private lives, and whether such tests produce reliable results is an awkward question. A conscientious employee is a careful, reliable one.

rely on · actual · facial expression · reaction · express · pick up (signals) · pay attention to · character traits · awkward · reliable · intrusion · apart from · conscientious
We rely as much on facial expression as on the actual words people say.
(rely on — depend on; facial expression — the look on someone's face)
Interviewers often pay particular attention to the way a candidate walks into the room.
(pay attention to — watch or listen carefully)

2. Thinking and opinions

When there is little hope, people resign themselves to the worst — they accept it. It can take a long time to convince someone who does not believe you. To regard something as important is to consider it important; in my view means "in my opinion". To be aware of something is to know about it, while a notion is a rough idea. Appointing your best friend as director is a clear case of favouritism. Someone can remind you of another person, or be obsessed with a hobby, and a group's mentality is its typical way of thinking.

resign oneself to · convince · regard ... as · in my view · be aware · notion · favouritism · remind someone of · be obsessed with · mentality
I have resigned myself to the worst.
(resign oneself to — accept something unpleasant)
In my view, this plan will not work.
(in my view — in my opinion)
Your new boyfriend reminds me of a cousin of mine.
(remind someone of — make you think of another person)

3. Expressions with 'feel'

Feel free to do something means you are welcome to do it. To have a feel for something is to have a natural talent or understanding for it. If you feel something in your bones, you are sure about it although you cannot explain why. You get the feel of something when you become used to doing it. Mixed feelings are feelings that pull in two directions; hard feelings are anger or resentment left after a quarrel; to hurt someone's feelings is to upset them. If you don't feel a thing, you feel no pain at all, and to feel your age is to feel as old as you really are.

feel free to · have a feel for · feel it in one's bones · get the feel of · mixed feelings · hard feelings · hurt someone's feelings · not feel a thing · feel one's age · know how someone feels
Feel free to have some tea or coffee.
(feel free to — you are welcome to)
I have mixed feelings about it.
(mixed feelings — feelings that pull in two directions)
I don't want to hurt her feelings.
(hurt someone's feelings — upset them)

4. Expressions with 'think' and verbs of communication

It's just a thought introduces a suggestion; to give something some thought is to consider it carefully; to be right in thinking is to be correct in believing. To think highly of someone is to respect them. On second thoughts means "after reconsidering". To think long and hard is to think very carefully, while someone who isn't thinking straight cannot think clearly. Verbs of communication: put (express in words), follow (understand what someone says), appreciate (be grateful — I would appreciate it if ...), utter (say), imply (suggest without saying directly), express (show or state), plead ignorance (pretend not to know), mislead (make someone believe the wrong thing) and spot (notice or recognise).

just a thought · give it some thought · think highly of · on second thoughts · think long and hard · put · follow · appreciate · utter · imply · express · plead ignorance · mislead · spot
He thinks very highly of you, so don't break his heart!
(think highly of — respect and admire)
On second thoughts, perhaps I'd better do it after all.
(on second thoughts — after reconsidering)

5. Verbs of strong feeling

Each verb has its own shade of meaning: regret (feel sorry about something you did), deplore (strongly disapprove of), resent (feel bitter about unfair treatment), dread (fear something that is going to happen), cherish (treasure and protect), mourn (grieve for someone who has died), offend (upset or insult), loathe (hate very much), stress (emphasise) and reproach (criticise someone for doing wrong).

regret · deplore · resent · dread · cherish · mourn · offend · loathe · stress · reproach
Peter regretted leaving his old job.
(regret — feel sorry about a past action)
She cherished the memory of her childhood in the country.
(cherish — treasure)

6. One word, several meanings

Familiar words change their meaning with the context. A spot can be a place (a nice spot for a picnic), a small amount (a spot of bother) or the only good part (the only bright spot). Mind can mean concentration (keep your mind on your work), memory (cast your mind back) or worries (a lot on my mind). Matter means a subject or problem (a personal matter; a matter of time — something that will certainly happen sooner or later). Dear means "loved" but also "expensive"; to hold on for dear life is to hold on as tightly as you can to survive. A hand is part of the body, but to have a hand in something is to be involved in it, in hand means being dealt with, and to raise your hand against someone is to hit them.

spot · mind · matter · dear · hand
It's only a matter of time before the city falls to the rebels.
(a matter of time — something certain to happen eventually)
As the boat lurched, we held on for dear life.
(for dear life — as tightly as possible, to survive)

⭐ Key points

  1. Reading feelings: rely on, facial expression, reaction to, pick up signals, pay attention to.
  2. Opinions: regard ... as, in my view, be aware, notion, convince, resign oneself to.
  3. 'Feel' expressions: feel free to, a feel for, mixed feelings, hard feelings, hurt someone's feelings.
  4. 'Think' expressions: think highly of, on second thoughts, think long and hard, that's a thought!
  5. Strong feelings: regret · deplore · resent · dread · cherish · mourn · offend · loathe · stress · reproach — each with its own shade.
  6. One word, several meanings: spot, mind, matter, dear and hand change meaning with context.
