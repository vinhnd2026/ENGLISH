1. Upbringing and family — fixed collocations

a spoilt child · a domineering father · live up to expectations · follow in someone's footsteps · pushy · put pressure on someone · in someone's best interests · rebel against · strike out on one's own · a sheltered upbringing · hit it off · pluck up courage · afraid of commitment · go through a bad patch · a trial separation

A spoilt child gets whatever he or she wants; a domineering parent tries to control everything; a sheltered upbringing means being over-protected as a child.

To live up to someone's expectations is to do as well as they hope; to follow in someone's footsteps is to take up the same career or way of life; a pushy person forces others to do what they want; to put pressure on someone is to make them feel they must succeed; something done in someone's best interests is done for that person's good.

To rebel against your upbringing is to reject the way you were raised; to strike out on your own is to become independent; to hit it off with someone is to like them immediately; to pluck up courage is to force yourself to be brave; to be afraid of commitment is to fear a long-term relationship; to go through a bad patch is to have a difficult period; a trial separation is a period of living apart to test a relationship.

I was a spoilt child, and I got whatever I wanted.
(a child who is given everything)
We hit it off immediately.
(liked each other at once)
He simply thought that he was acting in my best interests.
(for my good, as he saw it)

2. Words for people — relationships and stages of life

alien · fiancée · acquaintance · toddlers · the aged · ancestors · bachelor · best man · lad · relation

An alien is a foreigner in the legal sense — someone who must register with the police. This is different from an outsider (someone who does not belong to a group) and a stranger (someone you have never met).

A fiancée is a woman who is engaged to be married; the best man is the bridegroom's chief supporter at the wedding, who hands him the ring.

An acquaintance is someone you know slightly, not a close friend; a colleague is someone you work with.

Toddlers are one- and two-year-olds; the aged means old people in general; ancestors are the people you descend from — the opposite of descendants.

A bachelor is an unmarried man; a lad is a young man (informal); a relation is a member of your family (also called a relative).

Let me introduce you to my fiancée. We're getting married next month.
(the woman I am engaged to)
Our ancestors are all buried in the local churchyard.
(the generations before us)

3. Personality adjectives

conscientious · solitary · devoted · prejudiced · apathetic · aggressive · mature · attentive · extrovert · insensitive

Conscientious: hard-working and careful about doing things properly. Solitary: preferring to be alone. Devoted: loving and loyal (devoted to someone). Prejudiced: having unfair opinions formed in advance (prejudiced against a group). Apathetic: showing no interest in anything. Aggressive: quick to fight or argue. Mature: behaving in a sensible, adult way. Attentive: listening and taking care (attentive to people's needs). An extrovert: a lively, sociable person — the opposite of shy. Insensitive: not noticing or caring about other people's feelings.

Sharon works very hard and is extremely conscientious.
(careful and thorough)
Pauline is a good teacher, and very attentive to the needs of the students.
(she notices what they need)

4. Groups of people and family types

nearest and dearest · newlyweds · the nuclear family · adults · a community · a generation · contemporaries · the extended family · a household · outcasts

Your nearest and dearest are your closest family. Newlyweds are a couple who have only recently married. The nuclear family is parents and their children; adults are people who are no longer teenagers. A community is all the people living together in one area; a generation is all the people of approximately the same age; contemporaries are people who are alive at the same time, or who attend the same school. The extended family is the entire range of relatives in one family; a household is all the people who live together under the same roof; outcasts are people abandoned by their families or by society in general.

The nuclear family consists of parents and their children.
(the basic family unit)

5. Relationship verbs — care, conflict and parting

neglect · scold · offend · adopt · separated · quarrel · retire · criticise · abandon · humiliate

Neglect: to fail to look after someone. Scold: to tell a child off angrily. Offend: to hurt someone's feelings. Adopt: to take someone else's child into your family legally. Separated: living apart from your partner (be separated). Quarrel: to argue (quarrel with someone). Retire: to stop working at pension age. Criticise: to say what is wrong with someone. Abandon: to leave someone completely. Humiliate: to make someone feel ashamed in front of others.

turn someone down · keep in touch · grow up · go out together · fall out with · get on well with · run away from home · let someone down · move in with · get to know

To turn someone down is to refuse their offer or proposal; to keep in touch is to go on communicating; to grow up is to become an adult; to go out together is to have a romantic relationship; to fall out with someone is to quarrel and stop being friendly; to get on well with someone is to have a good relationship with them; to run away from home is to leave home without permission; to let someone down is to disappoint them; to move in with someone is to go and live in their home; to get to know someone is to become friendly with them over time.

Keith's parents neglected him badly when he was a baby.
(failed to look after him)
When Brian asked her to marry him, Ann turned him down.
(refused his proposal)
Helen got on well with her in-laws.
(had a good relationship with them)

6. One word, several uses

Took: took offence (was offended) · took to the job (started it confidently, as if he had always done it) · took the city (captured it after a siege).

Close: close to tears (nearly crying) · a close call (a very narrow win) · close and humid (hot and airless, of weather).

Shook: shook with rage (trembled) · shook the confidence of investors (damaged it) · shook its mane (moved it from side to side).

Steady: going steady (in a serious relationship) · Steady on! (wait — think again) · a steady decline (a slow, regular fall).

Leading: leading someone down the aisle (walking with them into church to be married) · leading the championship (in first place) · leading a horse (guiding it along).

After quarrelling with David, Martina was close to tears.
(nearly crying)
John and Mary have been going steady for almost five years.
(in a serious relationship)

⭐ Key points

  1. Upbringing collocations: a spoilt child, domineering (controlling), a sheltered upbringing (over-protected); live up to expectations, follow in someone's footsteps.
  2. Pressure and independence: put pressure on someone, in someone's best interests, rebel against, strike out on your own.
  3. Relationships, starting and struggling: hit it off, pluck up courage, afraid of commitment, go through a bad patch, a trial separation.
  4. Words for people are choosy: the aged (not ancient / elder), ancestors ≠ descendants, a relation is a family member, and an acquaintance is not a close friend.
  5. Personality adjectives: positive conscientious, devoted, attentive versus negative insensitive, prejudiced, apathetic, aggressive; also mature, extrovert, solitary.
  6. Relationship phrasal verbs: turn down, keep in touch, grow up, go out together, fall out with, get on well with, run away from home, let someone down, move in with, get to know — plus the multi-use words took, close, shook, steady, leading.
