Consolidation 3 — Modals, inversion and emphasis

There is no new theory in this unit — it consolidates four areas: modals in the present and future (Grammar 11), modals in the past (Grammar 12), inversion (Grammar 13) and emphasis (Grammar 14). The nine exercises below mix the areas together, so you have to recognise each pattern from the clues in the sentence — just as in real English use.

a) Modals — present and future

Review must, have to, should and ought to for obligation and advice; can, could, may and might for possibility; must and can't for certainty about the present; and patterns such as had better, be bound to, be likely to and may/might as well.

b) Modals — past

Modal + have + past participle is used to deduce and comment on past events: must have (certainly did), can't have (certainly didn't), should have (the right thing to do, but it didn't happen), needn't have (done, but unnecessary) — contrasted with didn't need to (not necessary, so not done).

c) Inversion

When a negative or restrictive adverbial comes first (Never, Rarely, Seldom, Little, Not until, Hardly… when, No sooner… than, Under no circumstances), the auxiliary verb moves in front of the subject. Inversion also replaces if in formal conditionals: Should you…, Had I known…, Were I to…

d) Emphasis

Cleft sentences (It was… that… and What… is…); fronting a sentence element for emphasis; do/did + bare infinitive; intensifiers such as very in my very own and at the very end; and concessive fronting in Strange as it may seem and Try as I might.

You should have told me the meeting was cancelled!
(should have — criticism of a past action)
Jean must have had a good time in Denmark.
(must have — certain deduction about the past)
Never have I seen a more boring film!
(inversion after Never)
Little did we know what was in store for us later!
(Little + inversion)
It was last week that I paid the bill.
(cleft sentence emphasising the time)
Strange as it may seem, Mary likes it here.
(concessive fronting with as)

⭐ Key points

  1. Deduction about the past: must have + past participle = certainly did; can't/couldn't have + past participle = certainly didn't; may/might/could have + past participle = possibly did.
  2. Should have + past participle: the right thing to do that didn't happen — regret or criticism; shouldn't have = it was wrong to do it.
  3. Needn't have + past participle (done, but unnecessary) is different from didn't need to (not necessary, and normally not done).
  4. Inversion: after Never, Rarely, Seldom, Little, Not until, Hardly, No sooner, Under no circumstances at the start of a clause — auxiliary + subject + verb (Not until … did I realise).
  5. Inversion in conditionals: Should + subject + verb, Had + subject + past participle, Were + subject + to-infinitive replace formal if-clauses.
  6. Emphasis: clefts It was … that… and What I need is…; do/did + infinitive; intensifying very (my very own, the very end) and the pattern Strange as it may seem.
