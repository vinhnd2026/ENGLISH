🔁 Review — What this unit covers

This consolidation unit has no new grammar. It brings together three areas: modal verbs in the present and future, modal verbs in the past, and functions (inviting, requesting, asking permission, asking the way, and so on).

a) Modal verbs — present and future

must / mustn't · don't have to · can / could · may / might · should / ought to · have to · be able to · be bound to

must expresses certainty (deduction) or obligation; mustn't is prohibition; don't have to means something is not necessary. may / might express possibility. should / ought to give advice or obligation. be able to completes can in other tenses. be bound to means something is certain to happen.

b) Modal verbs — past

must have + past participle · can't have + past participle · might/could have + past participle · should have + past participle · needn't have + past participle

must have = certainly did; can't have = certainly did not; might/could have = possibly did; should have = it was the right thing to do, but it was not done; needn't have = it was done but was not necessary — different from didn't need to (not necessary, and not done).

c) Functions

Would you like…? · Could you… please? · Is it all right if I…? · Could you tell me the way to…?

Inviting and offering: Would you like…?; polite requests: Could you… please?; asking permission: Is it all right if I…?; asking the way: Could you tell me the way to…?; asking for advice: What do you think I should do?

I should have taken a taxi.
You needn't have come early today.
Could you tell me the way to the station, please?
Would you like some tea?
Jack can't have left home yet.
Jean's plane might have been delayed.
You must be thirsty.
I'm bound to get lost.

⭐ Key points

  1. Deduction about the past: must have + past participle = certainly did; can't have + past participle = certainly did not; might/could have + past participle = possibly did.
  2. should have / ought to have + past participle = it was right to do it, but it was not done; needn't have + past participle = it was done but was not necessary.
  3. must (obligation / certainty) ≠ don't have to (not necessary) ≠ mustn't (prohibited).
  4. Polite functions: invite with Would you like…?, request with Could you… please?, ask permission with Is it all right if I…?
  5. Use be able to where can has no form (will be able to, was able to); be bound to = certain to happen.
