📚 Review — Consolidation 5

This is a consolidation unit: there is no new theory. The exercises below review the four most recent areas:

  • Comparisons (Grammar 24): comparatives and superlatives, as … as, such … as, the the more …, the … construction
  • Quantifiers (Grammar 23): all, no, none, each, every, either, neither and phrases with of
  • Articles (Grammar 22): a / an, the, and no article
  • Countable and uncountable nouns (Grammar 21): nouns such as furniture, information, traffic, and partitive phrases such as a slice of, a pair of
This is the best party I've ever been to.
(superlative + the)
Neither of the lifts was working.
(neither + singular verb)
Could you give me some information about the Arts Festival?
(information is uncountable)

⭐ Key points — Points to remember

  1. Uncountable nouns such as furniture, information, traffic, news, work are not used with a/an and have no plural -s. To talk about an amount, use a partitive phrase: a piece of information, an item of news.
  2. Partitives to learn: a slice of cake, a flight of stairs, a head of hair, a sheet of paper, a lump of coal, a glass of water, a helping of potatoes, a clap of thunder, a pair of shorts / trousers.
  3. Superlatives always take the: the best, the worst, the earliest; and remember the parallel pattern the more …, the …: The more slowly you walk, the longer it takes.
  4. Neither means “not one of two” (in an affirmative sentence); either has the same meaning in a negative sentence: I didn't talk to either of them.
  5. After most / some / none / all, use of + the before a specific noun: most of the first week, none of the museums.
