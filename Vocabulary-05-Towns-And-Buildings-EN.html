Vocabulary 5 — Towns and buildings

Towns and buildings: parts of a house · furniture · types of homes · the home/house family

1. Inside the house

a) Structure and rooms

attic · cellar · stairs · landing · chimney · fireplace

attic = the room under the roof at the top of the house; cellar = an underground room (for storing coal, wine, etc.); stairs = the steps inside a house (contrast steps, usually outside); landing = the flat area at the top of a flight of stairs; chimney = the pipe that carries smoke out; fireplace = the place in a room where a fire burns.

There is a beautiful stone fireplace in the living room.
At the top of the stairs there is a stained glass window.

b) Heating and floors

central heating · radiator · fitted carpet · rug

central heating = a system that warms the whole house; radiator = the wall panel it heats (you can dry wet socks on one); fitted carpet = a carpet fixed down to cover the whole floor; rug = a small loose carpet.

Put your wet socks on the radiator to dry.

2. Furniture and fittings

a) Furniture and seating

cupboards · bookshelves · armchair · settee / sofa · stool · wardrobe

cupboards = storage cupboards in the kitchen; bookshelves = shelves for books; armchair = a comfortable chair for one person; settee / sofa = a long seat for several people; stool = a small seat with no back or arms; wardrobe = a large cupboard for clothes.

b) Bedding and windows

duvet · blanket · sheets · pillow · cushion · curtains · blind · shutters · window sill

duvet = a thick warm bed cover; blanket = a thinner woollen cover; sheets = the cloths under and over you in bed; pillow = what you rest your head on; cushion = a soft pad to lean against; curtains = cloth hangings pulled across windows; blind = a screen pulled down to keep out the sun; shutters = wooden covers outside the windows; window sill = the flat ledge under a window.

Laura was sitting beside the fire in a comfortable armchair.

c) Small fittings

letterbox · door knocker · doormat · frame · ornaments

letterbox = the rectangular hole in the front door for letters; door knocker = the metal ring you knock to ask someone to open the door; doormat = the mat you wipe your feet on; frame = the border round a picture; ornaments = small decorative objects.

Mary has a lot of small ornaments on her window sill.

3. Kitchen, bathroom and garden

a) Kitchen and bathroom

cooker · oven · dishwasher · washing machine · sink · washbasin · shower · taps

cooker = the whole cooking appliance; oven = the enclosed part of it where food is baked or roasted; dishwasher = a machine that washes dishes; washing machine = a machine that washes clothes; sink = the basin in the kitchen; washbasin = the basin in the bathroom; shower = a spray of water you stand under; taps = the controls for water (mixer taps mix hot and cold).

Put the meat in the oven for two hours.

b) Garden and outside

gates · path · drive · fence · hedge · greenhouse · shed · lawn · lawnmower

gates = the doors at the entrance to the garden; path = a narrow stone way; drive = the short road from the street to the house or garage; fence = a wooden barrier; hedge = a living barrier of bushes; greenhouse = a glass house for plants; shed = a small storage hut in the garden; lawn = the area of grass; lawnmower = the machine that cuts it.

Peter was in the garden mowing the lawn.

4. Types of homes; home and house

a) Types of homes

detached house · semi-detached house · terraced house · cottage · bungalow · flat · block of flats

detached house = a house that shares no walls; semi-detached house = a house that shares one wall with the house next door; terraced house = one of a row of joined houses; cottage = a small house in the country; bungalow = a house with only one floor (good for older people who find stairs difficult); flat = a set of rooms in a bigger building; block of flats = the whole building of flats.

My house is semi-detached, and the neighbours often bang on the wall.

b) Areas

neighbourhood · suburb · countryside · housing estate · square · pedestrian street

neighbourhood = the area around your home; suburb = a residential area outside the centre; countryside = the country area with fields, woods and streams; housing estate = a planned area of new houses; square = an open area with a garden in the middle; a pedestrian street is for walkers only — no cars or lorries.

c) The home/house family

housework · housekeeper · homeless · hometown · houseboat · homesick · housing · at home

housework = washing, ironing and similar work in the home; housekeeper = a person employed to look after a house and children; homeless = without a home; hometown = the town you come from; houseboat = a boat used as a home; homesick = missing home; housing = homes in general (in housing estate); make yourself at home = relax and behave as if you were at home.

Pour yourself a drink and make yourself at home.

⭐ Key points

  1. Pairs not to confuse: a cooker is the appliance, a cook is the person (or the action); a sink is in the kitchen, a washbasin in the bathroom.
  2. stairs are inside a house; steps are usually outside. floor means both a storey (the first floor) and the surface you walk on; storey counts the levels of a building.
  3. Types of home: detached (no shared walls) · semi-detached (one shared wall) · terraced (joined in a row).
  4. furniture is uncountable: a lot of furniture, not *furnitures.
  5. The home family: hometown · homesick · homeless · at home; the house family: housework · housekeeper · houseboat · housing.
  6. Useful phrasal verbs: look out (look outside / be careful!) · knock down (demolish) · put out (take the rubbish out) · count on (rely on) · run out of (have none left) · get in (climb in) · worn out (exhausted).
