1. Common spelling problems

a) Words ending in -ful

useful · helpful — the suffix -ful has only ONE l  ·  adverbs: usefully · helpfully

When -ful is a suffix, it is written with only one l: useful, helpful. But when these adjectives add -ly to become adverbs, the result is usefully, helpfully.

A good map is very useful when you are travelling.

b) Doubling of consonants

swim → swimming · fit → fitted · thin → thinner

Words with one syllable ending in one vowel and one consonant double the final consonant when adding -ing, -ed or -er.

Most two-syllable words with the same ending also double it, especially when the stress is on the second syllable: prefer → preferred. If the stress is on the first syllable, there is no doubling: bother → bothering. A common exception is travel → traveller, which does double.

The children are swimming in the lake.
(swimming — the m is doubled)
He fitted the new lock on the door himself.
I preferred the first plan to the second one.

c) Words ending in -y

spy → spies · try → tries, tried · boy → boys · joy → joyful · shy → shyness

Nouns and verbs ending in consonant + y change -y to -i in plurals and past forms: spy → spies; try → tries, tried. Two-syllable words follow the same rule: reply → replies, replied; happy → happiness; beauty → beautiful.

But if the letter before -y is a vowel, nothing changes: boy → boys; destroy → destroyed. One-syllable words also keep the -y when adding -ful and -ness: joy → joyful; shy → shyness.

⚠️ These -y rules do not apply when adding -ing: try → trying; study → studying; annoy → annoying.
The spies were watching the house all night.
(spies — y changes to i)
She tried again and finally succeeded.
It was a joyful day for the whole family.
(joyful — the y does not change)
Their happiness was easy to see.
What a beautiful garden!
He is studying hard this week.
(studying — the y never changes before -ing)

d) Words with ie and ei

i before e, except after c — as long as the sound is /iː/: receive · relief

The general rule is i before e, except after c, as long as the sound is /iː/: receive, relief. When the sound is not /iː/, the rule does not apply, as in feign (pronounced /feɪn/).

Did you receive my letter yesterday?
(receive — ei after c)
She felt great relief when the exam ended.

2. Pronunciation: rhymes and vowel sounds

Two words rhyme when their ending sounds are exactly the same — even if the spelling is very different: hate rhymes with wait, and rough rhymes with stuff. The opposite trap is just as common: words that look alike can have completely different vowel sounds — in the group lost / post / toast / ghost / host, only lost sounds different. The surest method is to listen to each word and say it aloud.

The words "hate" and "wait" rhyme.
The words "rough" and "stuff" rhyme, although they are spelled very differently.

⭐ Key points

  1. The suffix -ful has only one l: useful, helpful — but the adverbs are usefully, helpfully.
  2. Words ending in one vowel + one consonant double the final consonant before -ing/-ed/-er (swim → swimming); so do two-syllable words stressed on the second syllable (prefer → preferred); familiar exception: traveller.
  3. Consonant + y: change y → i before -s/-ed (try → tries, tried; happy → happiness), but never before -ing (trying, studying).
  4. Vowel + y: the y stays (boy → boys, destroy → destroyed); one-syllable words also keep y before -ful/-ness (joyful, shyness).
  5. ie/ei: i before e, except after c — when the sound is /iː/ (receive, relief); the rule fails when the sound is different (feign).
  6. Spelling does not decide pronunciation: check each word's vowel sound and rhyme (lost differs from post / toast / ghost / host).
