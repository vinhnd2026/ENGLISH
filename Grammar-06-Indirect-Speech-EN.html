Reported speech: tense changes · reported questions · commands and requests · say / tell / ask · reporting verbs

1. Reported speech with tense changes

a) Tense changes after a past reporting verb

When the reporting verb is in a past tense (said, told, asked…), the tense of the original words usually moves back one step into the past. The Past Perfect stays the same, because it cannot move back any further.

'I agree.' — Peter said he agreed.
(present simple → past simple)
'I'm leaving.' — Jane said she was leaving.
(present continuous → past continuous)
'No, I hadn't forgotten.' — Greg said that he hadn't forgotten.
(the Past Perfect stays the same)

b) Complex sentences: only the first verb changes

In a sentence with several verbs, when we report it only the first verb changes tense; the following verbs keep the form that relates to it.

'I was walking home when I saw the accident.' — James said he had been walking home when he saw the accident.

c) Words for people, place and time change too

Because the point of reference changes, words such as here → there, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the day before, this → that must change as well.

'I'll see you here tomorrow, Jack,' said Mary. — Mary told Jack she would see him there the next day.
'I gave you this yesterday.' — John said he had given it to her the day before.

2. When the tense does NOT change

a) A present reporting verb

If the reporting verb is in a present tense, the reported words keep their original tense.

Brenda says she's arriving at about 6.00.

b) Things that are always true, or passed on immediately

If what is reported is something that is always true, or if the message is passed on immediately, the tense also stays the same.

Harry told me that he still likes you.
Mary said she's too busy to come.
(passed on immediately)

3. Reporting questions

a) Yes/No questions — use if

Yes/No questions are reported with if; the verb goes back to the normal statement form (no inversion) and there is no question mark.

'Do you like hamburgers?' — Charles asked me if I liked hamburgers.

b) Wh- questions — keep the question word

Questions with a question word (where, when, why, how…) keep that question word; the verb is also in statement form, with no question mark.

'Where are we going?' — I asked Sue where we were going.

c) Polite questions

To ask for information politely, we use phrases such as Could you tell me…? or Do you know…? — note that what follows still uses statement word order. When reporting this kind of question, we still use ask and the usual tense-change rules.

'Where is the station?' → 'Could you tell me where the station is?' → I asked him where the station was.

4. Commands, requests and reporting verbs

a) Commands — tell + object + to-infinitive; Requests — ask + object + to-infinitive

'Go away!' — He told me to go away.
'Please help me.' — He asked her to help him.

b) Say or tell?

We say something (say something, optionally with to + the listener), and we tell somebody (tell somebody) — never put to between tell and its object: I said to him that… but I told him that…

c) Other reporting verbs

Each reporting verb has its own pattern, which must be learned verb by verb (a good dictionary shows the patterns):

She suggested going to the beach. · She offered to give me a lift. · She reminded me to call my mother.
(suggest + V-ing · offer + to-infinitive · remind + object + to-infinitive)

d) Reporting the main idea (paraphrase)

It is not always necessary (or possible) to report every word; usually we only need to convey the main idea of what was said.

'Excuse me, do you think you could tell me the time?' — He asked me what the time was.

⭐ Key points

  1. After a past reporting verb, the tense usually moves back (present → past, past → past perfect; the Past Perfect stays the same).
  2. Words of time and place also change to match the new point of reference (tomorrow → the next day, here → there…).
  3. There are two kinds of reported question: Yes/No questions reported with if, and Wh- questions with the question word; the verb is not in question form and there is no question mark.
  4. A reported sentence usually begins with a reporting verb; each verb is followed by different grammatical patterns — a good dictionary gives this information.
  5. A report can also be a paraphrase of the main idea of what was said, not the exact words.
