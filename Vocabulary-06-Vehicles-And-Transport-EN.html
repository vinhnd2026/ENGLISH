Vocabulary 6 — Vehicles and transport

Vehicles and transport: people · parts of a car · places · the sail/ship family

1. People on the move

chauffeur · motorist · driver · captain · guard · traffic warden · steward · crew · conductor · cyclist · pedestrian · mechanic

chauffeur = a paid driver of a smart private car; motorist = a car driver in general; driver = anyone who drives a vehicle; captain = the person in command of a ship or plane; guard = the train official who waves the green flag; traffic warden = an official who gives parking tickets; steward = an attendant on a plane or ship; crew = all the people working on a ship or plane; conductor = the person who takes fares on a bus; cyclist = a person who rides a bicycle; pedestrian = a person walking; mechanic = a person who repairs cars in a garage.

Before take-off, the steward told me to fasten my seat belt.
The bus mounted the pavement and injured a pedestrian.

2. Parts of a car

a) Outside

roof rack · aerial · boot · exhaust · wheel · wing · tyre · bumper · headlight · bonnet · windscreen · wiper · mirror

roof rack = the frame on top for luggage; aerial = the rod that receives radio signals; boot = the luggage space at the back (British); exhaust = the pipe for waste gases; wheel = one of the round parts the car runs on; wing = the body panel above a wheel; tyre = the rubber ring round a wheel; bumper = the protective bar at the front or back; headlight = a powerful front light; bonnet = the cover over the engine; windscreen = the front window; wiper = the blade that cleans the windscreen; mirror = the glass for seeing behind.

Your car engine is usually under the bonnet.

b) Inside; bicycles

steering wheel · gear · brakes · handlebars · chain

steering wheel = the wheel the driver turns; gear = you change it to change speed; brakes = what you use to stop; handlebars = what you hold when riding a bicycle; chain = the bicycle's metal link band (also a necklace).

The pilot of a plane sits in the cockpit.

3. Places and journeys

platform · quay · runway · pavement · timetable · coach · tunnel · ticket office · railway line · filling station · deck · cockpit

platform = where you wait for a train; quay = where ships load at a port; runway = where planes take off and land; pavement = the raised path at the side of a road; timetable = the list of train times; coach = a railway carriage or a long-distance bus; tunnel = a passage under the ground; ticket office = where you buy tickets; railway line = the railway route; filling station = where you buy petrol; deck = the open top floor of a ship; cockpit = where the pilot sits.

The platform was crowded with passengers waiting for the train.
Everything went dark when the train entered a tunnel.

4. The sail/ship family and phrasal verbs

a) The sail/ship family

sailor · sail · sails · sailed · sailing · ship · shipped · shipment · shipping

sailor = a person who works on a ship; set sail = to start a voyage; go for a sail = to go out in a sailing boat for pleasure; sails = the large cloths that catch the wind; ship (verb) = to send goods; shipped = sent by ship; shipment = a load of goods being sent; shipping clerk = the office worker in a harbour who deals with shipments; aboard ship = on the ship.

We're all flying home but our furniture is being shipped.

b) Phrasal verbs

take up · pick up · put up with · keep up with · show off · put out · try out · deal with · make for · look forward to

take up = to occupy space; pick up = to lift and carry away; put up with = to tolerate; keep up with = to stay at the same speed as; show off = to display something proudly to attract attention; put out = to extinguish a fire; try out = to test something new; deal with = to handle a problem; make for = to head towards; look forward to = to wait for with pleasure.

How can you put up with all those exhaust fumes!

⭐ Key points

  1. driver is the general word for a person who drives; a motorist drives a car; a chauffeur is a paid private driver.
  2. British car words: boot (luggage space) · bonnet (engine cover) · windscreen (front window) · wing (panel over the wheel) · tyre.
  3. coach means both a railway carriage and a long-distance bus — check the context.
  4. On board: you fall overboard into the water, you go aboard a ship, and a boat sets sail.
  5. As a verb, ship means to send goods: a shipment is a load of goods, shipping is the transport of goods.
  6. Transport phrasal verbs: take up (occupy space) · keep up with (stay at the same speed) · put out (extinguish) · try out (test) · deal with (handle).
