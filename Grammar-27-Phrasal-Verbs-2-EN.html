1. Verbs with one particle: transitive and separable

These phrasal verbs take an object. The object can come after the particle or between the verb and the particle — so the verbs are separable:

V + particle + O  ·  V + O + particle
We brought up this child. — We brought her up.
(long object phrases usually go after the particle; pronouns, like “her”, usually go in the middle)

Two exceptions are call back and show around: with these verbs, the object is always put between the verb and the particle.

Let me show you around the new building.

Verbs marked with an asterisk * have a second meaning. With this meaning they have an intransitive form (no object) — see card 2.

  • bring up — look after until adult
  • carry out — act upon
  • call off — cancel
  • clear up* — make tidy; (weather) improve
  • fill in* — complete by writing; take someone’s place
  • find out — learn about, discover
  • give away — make a gift of; make something known
  • give up* — stop doing something; stop trying
  • hold up — rob
  • knock out — make unconscious
  • leave out — not include
  • look up* — find information; (things) appear better
  • make up* — invent; become friends again
  • pick up — collect
  • put aside — save
  • put off — postpone; make someone not want to do something
  • put up — provide accommodation
  • show around — give a guided tour
  • take over* — buy a company; take someone’s place
  • take up — start a hobby
  • tear up — tear into pieces
  • think over — consider
  • try out — use for the first time
  • turn down — refuse an offer
  • wear out — make tired
  • work out — find a solution
You haven’t carried out my instructions.
We have decided to call off the match.
The millionaire gave all his money away.
I’ll pick you up at six.
Paul was offered the job but he turned it down.
This is a difficult problem. I can’t work it out.

2. Verbs with one particle: intransitive

These phrasal verbs have no object. Verbs marked with an asterisk * have a second meaning; with this meaning they have a transitive form.

  • break down — stop working
  • break out — begin suddenly
  • come out — be published
  • draw up* — come to a stop; write a legal document
  • fall out — quarrel
  • get away — escape
  • give in — stop fighting against
  • go off — explode
  • look out — beware
  • set in — arrive and stay
  • show off — behave to attract attention
  • take off — leave the ground
  • turn up* — arrive, often unexpectedly; increase
  • wear off — become less strong or disappear
The car broke down on the motorway.
The war broke out unexpectedly.
Her new book came out last week.
Your plane takes off at 6.00.
Guess who turned up at our party?
When the drug wears off you may feel pain.

⭐ Key points

  1. When you meet a new phrasal verb, check it in a dictionary to see how it is used.
  2. Many phrasal verbs have more than one meaning — use the context to choose the right one.
  3. Phrasal verbs tend to be more common in spoken language and informal writing than in formal writing.
