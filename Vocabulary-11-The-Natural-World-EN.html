1. Landscape and water

bank — the side of a river or lake. The fixed phrase burst its banks means the river overflows. Compare: edge = the outer rim of something; side = one of the surfaces or parts of something.

puddle — a small pool of water left after rain; flood — a large amount of water covering land; lake — a large area of water surrounded by land.

cave — a hollow place in rock; cliff — a steep rock face; valley — low land between hills; slope — a surface that slants.

stream — a small, narrow river; river — a large natural flow of water; torrent — a violent, fast stream of water.

waves — the raised lines of water on the surface of the sea or a lake; tide — the regular rise and fall of the sea. Waters is not used to mean "waves".

top / peak / summit — all mean the highest point: top is the most general, peak is the pointed top of a mountain, and summit is formal, for the very highest point.

desert — dry land with little rain; plain — a large flat area of land; sand — tiny grains of rock found on beaches and in deserts. fountain — a jet of water thrown into the air; source — where a river or information comes from; tap — the device you turn to get water indoors.

cloudy — full of clouds (the adjective from cloud); lightning — the flash of light in a storm; thunder — the loud noise after lightning; hurricane — a violent storm.

canal — a man-made waterway for boats; a channel is usually a stretch of sea between two areas of land, or a TV station.

After the heavy rain, the river burst its banks and flooded the fields.
From the top of the hill you can see the sea.

2. Animals

Typical animal actions and sounds: slither — slide along the ground (snake); spin a web — make a net of threads (spider); buzz — fly around with a humming sound (wasp, bee); pinch — squeeze between claws (crab); croak — make a deep, rough sound (frog); quack — make the sound of a duck; moo — make the sound of a cow; baa — make the sound of a sheep; miaow — make the sound of a cat; bark — make the short, loud sound of a dog; wag its tail — move its tail from side to side.

herd — a group of farm animals; flock — a group of sheep or birds; cattle — cows kept on a farm (always plural).

breed — a type of animal developed by people (a kind of dog, cat…); tame — used to people, not wild or afraid; wildlife — wild animals in general; nest — the home a bird builds; kennel — a small house for a dog; mosquitoes — small flying insects that bite.

A large green snake was slithering across the floor towards me.
The dog wagged its tail furiously when it saw the children.
Some wild animals will become tame if they get used to people.
You have to sleep under a net to avoid being bitten by mosquitoes.

3. Plants and trees

Parts of a tree: roots — the parts under the ground; trunk — the thick main stem; branch — a large arm of the tree; twig — a very small, thin branch; bark — the hard outer covering; leaves — the flat green parts (singular: leaf).

forest — a large area of trees; a pine forest — a forest of pine trees; lawn — an area of short, carefully cut grass (in a garden or park); grass — the plant in general.

stone — the hard seed inside some fruits (cherry, peach…); seeds — the small grains you plant to grow new plants; peel — the skin of a fruit (orange, banana), also a verb meaning "take the skin off"; plant — a living thing that grows in the ground, often in a pot, also a verb meaning "put in the ground to grow".

corn — grain crops; in this unit, field after field of golden corn waving in the wind.

We cut down the tree, but then we had to dig up its roots.

4. Word formation, compounds and phrasal verbs

Common word building: child → childhood (the suffix -hood shows a period or state); suit → unsuitable (the negative prefix un-); destroy → destruction, protect → protection (-tion); lonely → loneliness (-ness); hunt → hunter (-er for a person); wood → wooden (-en for material); obey → disobedient (dis-); discover → discovery; save → savings (money you have saved — always plural).

Compounds in this unit: mousetrap, lawn mower, drinking fountain, weather forecast, hillside, mountaintop, farmhouse, beehive, rabbit hole, snakeskin.

Phrasal verbs: go off — go bad (food, milk); mind out — be careful, watch out; let off — release from a lead; be put off by — be discouraged by something; get used to + V-ing — become familiar with something; find out — discover information; tear up — tear into small pieces; take to + V-ing — start liking something or doing it as a habit.

I think this fruit juice has gone off. It smells funny.
Our dog never got used to sleeping in its kennel.
There's a drinking fountain on the other side of the park.

⭐ Key points

  1. bank is the side of a river — burst its banks is a fixed phrase; an edge is a rim, a side is one part or face of something.
  2. peak / summit / top all mean the highest point: top is the most general, peak is a pointed mountain top, and summit is the most formal.
  3. Animal sounds and actions: a dog barks and wags its tail; a duck quacks; a wasp buzzes; a frog croaks; a cow moos; a sheep baas; a cat miaows; a snake slithers; a spider spins a web; a crab pinches.
  4. Parts of a tree: roots, trunk, branch (large), twig (small), bark, leaves. A stone is the hard seed inside some fruits; peel is the skin of a fruit.
  5. Word building: -hood (childhood), un- (unsuitable), -tion (destruction, protection), -ness (loneliness), -er (hunter), -en (wooden), dis- (disobedient); savings is always plural.
  6. Phrasal verbs: go off (go bad), mind out (be careful), let off (release from a lead), be put off by (be discouraged by), get used to + V-ing, find out (discover), tear up (tear into pieces), take to + V-ing (start liking).
