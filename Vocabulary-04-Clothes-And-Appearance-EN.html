1. Names of clothes and accessories

More words from the exercises: uniform · formal clothes · sleeves · dressing gown (worn at home over pyjamas) · overalls (protective work clothes) · mac (a light raincoat) · scarf · shoelaces · pockets · aprons · bow tie

At Harry's school, the children have to wear a special uniform.
When it snows, Freda always wears a scarf around her neck.

2. Putting on, taking off; fit and suit

put on ↔ take off  ·  try on (to see if it fits)  ·  undo (buttons, zips)

fit = be the right size; suit = look good on a person; go with = match another item. Alterations: have something taken in (made smaller), let out (made bigger), let down (made longer), taken up (made shorter). Also: plain (no pattern), inside out, outfit (a set of clothes worn together), costume (for fancy dress or the stage).

This jacket is the kind of thing I want. Can I try it on?
I don't think that purple shirt goes with your yellow skirt.
Those trousers are far too big. Why don't you have them taken in?
You look hot in that coat. Why don't you take it off?

3. Word formation and common mistakes

The suit family: suit → suitable → unsuitable. The prefix un-: undress, unbuttoned, unsuitable, informal. Also: appearance, parting (where hair is divided), striped, fashionable, worn (wear – wore – worn), cleaner's (the dry-cleaner's shop).

⚠️ Common mistakes: clothes are measured in size, not number; clothes = the things you wear, cloth = material; trousers is always plural (were they?); for a person in a photograph use the present continuous is wearing; and you take off your clothes, not put them out.
This shirt is too small; it's not my size.
As far as I can see, the man in this photograph is wearing a suit.
Joan was dressed completely in white.

4. Phrasal verbs in this unit

roll up (your sleeves) · let down (make a skirt/trousers longer) · give somebody away (reveal who they are) · fall over · get at (suggest, mean) · put aside (save money) · go in for (enjoy doing) · look around (the shops)

If I wear a long-sleeved shirt, I usually roll up the sleeves.
I'm putting aside money every week to buy a new sports jacket.
The thief wore gloves so that his fingerprints didn't give him away.

⭐ Key points

  1. fit = be the right size; suit = look good on the person; go with = match another item.
  2. put on ↔ take off; try on = wear something to see if it fits; undo = open buttons/zips.
  3. Alterations: taken in (smaller), let out (bigger), let down (longer), taken up (shorter).
  4. trousers is always plural; clothes (what you wear) is not cloth (material); clothes are measured in size.
  5. For a person in a photograph, use the present continuous: is wearing.
