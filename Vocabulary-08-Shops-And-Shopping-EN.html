1. Shops and shoppers

bargain — something bought for less than its real value; the sales are the period when shops sell goods cheaply (buy cheaply in the sales).

for sale — offered to be bought; on sale — being sold at a reduced price. Do not confuse the two.

shopper — a person who is shopping; a customer is a person who buys from a particular shop or business.

change — the money you get back when you pay with more than the exact price.

receipt — the piece of paper that proves you bought something; you usually need it to return goods. Do not confuse it with recipe (instructions for cooking).

pricey — rather expensive; prices are the amounts goods are sold for (the prices are very high), while costs are usually expenses.

discount — a reduction in price (a 10 per cent discount); a purchase is something you buy, or the act of buying (a carrier bag free with each purchase over £10).

pay for something — give money for it (How much did you pay for your new shoes?); spend — use money (spend money on something).

That new clothes shop has a lot of very good bargains.
It costs £9, so give her £10, and she'll give you £1 change.
(change = the £1 you get back)
If you pay cash, we can give you a 10 per cent discount.

2. In the shop

queue — wait in a line; serve — help a customer in a shop; help — "Can I help you?"; pay — give the money at the cash desk.

fit — be the right size: "it doesn't fit me"; try on — put clothes on to see if they fit; change — exchange something for another size or item; wrap — cover a present with paper.

deliver — bring goods to your home; order — ask a shop to get something for you (we can order it for you); find — manage to get exactly what you wanted.

Kinds of shop: department store — a large shop selling many kinds of goods; shopping centre — a group of shops under one roof; corner shop — a small local shop; filling station — a place that sells petrol; cash register — the machine that records payments; shop assistant — a person who works in a shop; shopping list — a list of things to buy; bargain hunting — looking for cheap goods.

Goods words: stall — a table or small open shop in a market; goods — things that are sold; label — the small piece of material or paper on goods with information (washing instructions are on the label); make — the name of the company that made something, e.g. a car: "What make is it?"; out of stock — not available because everything has been sold; available — ready to be bought.

Containers and amounts: a tube of toothpaste, a bunch of flowers, a pot of jam, a tin of baked beans, a box of chocolates / paper tissues, a packet of breakfast cereal, a bar of soap, a carton of orange juice, a tub of margarine.

You have to queue for ages to pay in this supermarket.
(queue for ages = wait in line for a very long time)
I like the colour of this skirt, but it doesn't fit me.
We don't have the CD, I'm afraid. It's out of stock.

3. Phrasal verbs and word formation

make out — manage to see or read something; try on — put on clothes to check the size; hold up — rob a place using force or threats; grow out of — become too big to wear something.

worn out — exhausted; pick up — collect something (or someone); run out of — have nothing left of something; call in (at) — visit a place on your way somewhere; put up with — tolerate something unpleasant; think over — consider something carefully before deciding.

Common word formation: variety (from VARY), shortage (SHORT), likelihood (LIKELY), frozen (FREEZE), successful (SUCCESS), expensive (EXPENSE), reduction (REDUCE), complaints (COMPLAIN), impolite (POLITE, with the negative prefix im-), willingness (WILLING).

I can't make out what the price is.
Two masked men held up the supermarket yesterday.
Sorry, we have run out of bread.
I'll think it over.

⭐ Key points

  1. bargain = something bought cheaply; the sales = the period of reduced prices; for sale = offered to buyers, on sale = at a reduced price.
  2. receipt = proof of buying (not recipe = cooking instructions); change = money given back.
  3. pay for something; spend money (on something).
  4. out of stock = sold out; available = ready to buy; fit = be the right size; stall = a market stand; goods = things sold.
  5. Phrasal verbs: make out, try on, hold up, grow out of, run out of, pick up, call in, put up with, think over; worn out = exhausted.
  6. Containers: a tube of, a bunch of, a pot of, a tin of, a box of, a packet of, a bar of, a carton of, a tub of.
