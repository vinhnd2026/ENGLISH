Grammar 28 — Verbs followed by -ing or infinitive

Verbs + -ing · Verbs + to-infinitive · Two forms, two meanings · Verbs of perception

1. Verbs followed by -ing

a) Followed by an -ing form or a noun

V + V-ing / V + noun:  avoid · be worth · dislike · enjoy · fancy · help · keep · mind · miss · practise · risk · can't stand

These verbs are never followed by a to-infinitive. After them comes either an -ing form or a noun.

Try to avoid walking as much as possible.
It's not worth waiting for a bus at this time of day.
I don't fancy going out this evening.
I can't stand going to office parties.

b) Followed by an -ing form, a noun, or a that-clause

admit · consider · deny · imagine · recollect · report · suggest

This group has three patterns: V + V-ing, V + noun, or V + that + clause.

Jack admitted stealing the money.
Peter denied stealing the money.
I suggested that we went to the beach.

2. Verbs followed by -ing or the infinitive — little change of meaning

attempt · begin · continue · dread · not bear · hate · intend · like · love · prefer · start

These verbs can be followed by either form, with little or no change in meaning.

I attempted to leave but the police stopped me.
(attempted leaving is also possible)

would like / would love / would prefer + to-infinitive

These three expressions are followed by the to-infinitive only, never by an -ing form.

I'd like to come to your party, but I'll be away then.

like to for a habitual choice

As well as its normal meaning of something that gives pleasure, like to can describe a habitual action, whether or not it gives us pleasure.

On Sundays I like to get up early and go for a swim.

3. Verbs followed by -ing or the infinitive — change of meaning

With these six verbs, the meaning changes with the form that follows. Learn each pair carefully.

forget / remember

forget / remember + V-ing = a memory of the past  ·  + to V = something you are supposed to do
I won't forget meeting you.
(meet first — remember afterwards)
Please remember to lock the door.
(remember first — lock afterwards)

go on

go on + V-ing = continue doing the same thing  ·  go on + to V = move on to something else
Diana went on working all night.
(did the same thing)
The director went on to say that the strike was over.
(did something else)

mean

mean + V-ing = involve / have as a result  ·  mean + to V = intend
This means leaving at 6.00.
I meant to phone you but I forgot.

regret

regret + V-ing = be sorry about something in the past  ·  regret + to tell / to inform = give bad news formally
Kate regretted not buying the house.
I regret to tell you that you have failed.
(a formal use)

stop

stop + V-ing = end an action  ·  stop + to V = stop in order to do something else
I stopped going to evening classes.
(gave up going)
I stopped to buy some coffee.
(in order to buy)

try

try + V-ing = do something and see what happens  ·  try + to V = make an effort (you may not succeed)
Why don't you try getting up early?
(suggesting an action)
I tried to get up early, but I couldn't.
(try and fail)

4. Verbs followed by the infinitive

a) Followed by the to-infinitive only

afford · appear · ask · choose · fail · happen · help · long · manage · offer · prepare · refuse · tend · wait · want
I can't afford to go on holiday abroad this year.
Gerry failed to arrive on time.

b) Followed by the infinitive or a that-clause

agree · arrange · decide · demand · desire · expect · hope · intend · learn · plan · pretend · promise · seem · threaten · wish
Tom agreed to meet us outside the cinema.
John decided that he would take the bus.

c) Verbs of perception

feel · hear · listen to · notice · see · watch  + object + V-ing / infinitive without to

If we see or hear only part of the action, or the action is continuing, we use the -ing form. If we see or hear the whole action from beginning to end, we use the infinitive without to.

I felt the train moving.
(continuing action)
I felt the train move.
(one completed action)
⚠️ With a that-clause, these verbs change meaning: feel that = believe, hear that = receive news, see that = make sure something is done.
I feel that you should look for another job.
(= believe)

⭐ Key points

  1. Check new verbs in a good dictionary: it will show whether the verb is followed by an -ing form, a to-infinitive, or both — the surest way to use it correctly.
  2. Some verbs can be used in different ways with changes of meaning: stop doing ≠ stop to do, try doing ≠ try to do.
  3. would like / would love / would prefer are always followed by the to-infinitive, never by an -ing form.
  4. Verbs of perception: use the -ing form for an action in progress or part of an action, and the infinitive without to for the whole action.
