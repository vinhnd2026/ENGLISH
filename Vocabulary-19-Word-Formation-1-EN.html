Word formation 1: prefixes · suffixes · compound words · verb phrases with make / do / take…

1. Prefixes — added to the beginning of a word

A prefix is added to the beginning of a word to make a new word. It usually changes the meaning: many prefixes are negative, or mean “wrongly”, “too much”, “again”, “together”, and so on. When you meet a long word, try splitting off the prefix to guess its meaning.

dis- · un- · in- · mis- · over- · re- · non- · vice- · trans- · sub- · pre- · multi- · co- · out-
The children never do what I tell them to! They are very disobedient.
If you co-operate with the police you will receive a light sentence.
Mr Jones is incredibly rich. In fact he's a multi-millionaire.

2. Suffixes — added to the end of a word

A suffix is added to the end of a word. Unlike a prefix, a suffix usually changes the word class: it makes nouns, adjectives, nouns for people, or adverbs. This is the fastest way to grow your vocabulary: knowing one base word means knowing a whole word family.

-dom · -ful · -ian · -age · -ing · -ment · -ish · -wards · -less · -ship · -ee
I don't want to be a slave! I demand my freedom.
How kind of you to bring flowers! That was very thoughtful.
Martin plays the guitar, but he isn't a very good musician.

3. Compound words

A compound word is made when two separate words join to form a new word with its own meaning. Compounds may be written as one word (gunfire) or as two words (soap powder, crash helmet) — and their meaning cannot always be guessed from the parts.

gunfire · thunderstorm · handwriting · haircut · soap powder · crash helmet · bookcase · rainbow · footsteps · fireplace
We had to take shelter during a severe thunderstorm.
Nobody can read the doctor's handwriting.
At one end of the room is a lovely old stone fireplace.

4. Fixed verb phrases with make, do, take…

Some verbs combine with a fixed noun (a collocation) and change their form with the tense. They cannot be translated word by word; learn the whole phrase: make a noise, feel like, take your time, set foot on, give permission, do a favour, put the blame on, fall in love, draw attention to.

make a noise · feel like · take your time · set foot on · give permission · do a favour · put the blame on · fall in love · draw attention to
You don't have to hurry. You can take your time.
Armstrong was the first person to set foot on the Moon.
When something goes wrong, people always put the blame on me!

⭐ Key points

  1. Prefixes change the meaning of the base word: dis-/un-/in- negative, mis- wrongly, over- too much, re- again, non- not, vice- deputy, trans- across, sub- under, pre- before/in advance, multi- many, co- together.
  2. Suffixes usually change the word class: -dom, -age, -ment, -ship make nouns; -ful, -less, -ish make adjectives; -ian, -ee make nouns for people; -wards makes adverbs of direction.
  3. Compounds may be written as one word (gunfire) or two (soap powder, crash helmet); the meaning cannot always be guessed from the parts.
  4. Learn verb phrases as wholes and change the verb with the tense: making a noise, gave permission, has fallen in love…
  5. One base word can take several prefixes/suffixes: satisfy → satisfied → dissatisfied; charge → overcharged; employ → employee; home → homeless.
