Grammar 8 — Wishes and Related Forms

Wish sentences: I wish · If only · It's time · I'd rather · Suppose / Imagine

1. Wish — sentences with I wish

a) Wishes about the present — I wish + Past Simple

I wish + S + V-ed / V₂  ·  the time referred to is an imagined present

For a wish that is contrary to the facts now, use I wish + a verb in the past simple form, even though the time referred to is an imagined present.

I wish I knew the answer to this question.
(In reality, I do not know it.)
I wish I didn't have so much work to do.
(In reality, I have a great deal of work.)

b) Wishes about the past — I wish + Past Perfect

I wish + S + had + V₃

For a wish or regret about something already past, use I wish + the past perfect.

I wish I had gone to your party last week.
(In reality, I did not go.)

c) Future wishes and impossible things — I wish + could

For something in the future we use could; could is also used for things that are generally difficult or impossible. A future wish can also be expressed with have to.

I wish June could meet me next week.
I wish I could drive.
I wish I didn't have to get up early tomorrow.

d) Complaints and wanting things to happen — I wish + would

Use I wish + would when complaining about a bad habit, or talking about something you want to happen.

I wish Peter wouldn't chew gum all the time.
I wish the police would do something about these people!

2. Related structures: If only · It's time · I'd rather · Suppose/Imagine

a) If only — a stronger form of I wish

I wish can be replaced by If only to emphasise the wish; in speech, only is usually stressed.

If only I knew the answer to this question!
If only I had gone to your party last week!

b) It's time + a past form

It's time + S + V-ed / V₂  ·  It's high time … (stronger)

After It's time I/you/we… we use a past form; the meaning is close to a second conditional (“if … it would be better”). Adding high makes it stronger.

Sorry, but it's time we went home.
It's high time you learned to look after yourself!

It's time is also used with to + infinitive, but the meaning is slightly different — it simply states a fact, with no hint of a reminder:

It's time to start work.
(Just a statement of fact; compare It's time you started work! — a reminder to someone who is being lazy.)

c) I'd rather + a past form

I'd rather + S + V-ed / V₂

After I'd rather I/you/we… we also use a past form, with a meaning close to a second conditional.

I'd rather you didn't tell John about this.

d) Suppose / Imagine

In informal speech, suppose or imagine can replace if; the structure is an ordinary second conditional.

Suppose you lost your keys. What would you do?
Imagine you were rich. How would you feel?

⭐ Key points

  1. Wishes about the present use the past simple form; wishes about the past use the past perfect.
  2. I wish + would: complaining about an annoying habit, or wanting something to happen.
  3. After It's time and I'd rather, use a past form for an imagined situation: It's time we left.
  4. I hope … is also used like I wish …, but only for wishes that can really happen, and it usually carries a good meaning: I hope you have a good time. Wish cannot be used in this way.
  5. I hope + to V: I hope to see you next week. I wish + to V, by contrast, is a formal way of saying “I want / I would like to”: I wish to interview you for the job next week.
  6. Common wishing formulas: I wish you luck / success in your new job. — We wish you a happy New Year.
