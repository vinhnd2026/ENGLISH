Feelings and opinions: feeling adjectives · near-synonyms · body language · giving opinions · character · word formation

1. Feeling adjectives — -ed or -ing?

Adjectives ending in -ing describe the person or thing that causes a feeling; adjectives ending in -ed describe the feeling of the person affected. Common feeling words:

interested · interesting · annoyed · annoying · afraid · anxious · scared · angry · cross · furious · nervous · upset · careless

Use afraid for a specific fear (spiders, missing a plane); anxious is general worry. Cross is milder than angry; furious is the strongest — losing your temper completely (lose your temper).

Sue wasn't really interested in the film.
(the film did not hold her attention)
Tim completely lost his temper! He was absolutely furious.
(furious = extremely angry)
You're not scared of spiders, are you?
(scared = frightened)

2. Near-synonyms — differences of strength

Many feeling adjectives have near-synonyms that are weaker or stronger. Learn them in pairs:

badly-behaved = naughty · frightened = scared · unhappy = upset · boring = dull · interesting → fascinating (much stronger) · happy = glad · miserable → depressed (deeper) · mixed up = confused
Jean was very upset when her kitten was run over.
(upset = unhappy and worried)
I'm so glad that Helen has got the job she wanted.
(glad = pleased and happy)

3. Body language — actions that show feelings

Each body action is linked to a particular feeling or attitude:

blush (when embarrassed) · yawn (when tired or bored) · nod your head (to show agreement) · grin (when amused or pleased) · cry (when upset) · shake your head (to show disagreement) · scream (when scared or in pain) · wave (to attract attention)
In reply to Jack's questions, Sue shook her head.
(she was showing disagreement)
When you feel embarrassed you might blush.
(blush = go red in the face)

4. Giving opinions

Useful phrases for giving your opinion or reacting to someone else's:

In my opinion, … · as far as I'm concerned · to be honest · be keen on · I didn't think much of it · Mind your own business! · It just won't do. · It's not worth it. · Never mind, cheer up!
I'm not very keen on that kind of thing, to be honest.
(keen on = liking something a lot)
You can do whatever you like, as far as I'm concerned.
(as far as I'm concerned = in my view)

5. Character adjectives

A group of adjectives describing people's character — learn each with the behaviour it suggests:

realistic (faces facts) · imaginative (makes up stories) · obedient (carries out instructions) · determined (doesn't give up easily) · tolerant (puts up with differences) · dependable (people can count on you) · quarrelsome (keeps falling out with people) · helpful (puts yourself out for others) · embarrassed (can't stand people looking at you) · co-operative (gets on well working with others)
If you are determined, you don't give up easily.
Julie always thought of herself as an easy going and tolerant person.
(easy going = relaxed and not easily worried)

6. Word formation & prepositions

The word family from the film review:

IMPRESS → impression · OFFEND → offensive · IRRITATE → irritating · ADMIRE → admiration · COWARD → cowardice · ADORE → adorable · ATTRACT → attraction · GRATEFUL → gratitude · FAITH → faithful · RELIEVE → relief

Fixed prepositions and phrases to learn by heart:

ashamed of · fond of · get into trouble · laugh at · cross with · believe in · fed up with · keen on · shake her/his head · cheer up
Do you like chocolate cake? I am very fond of it.
(fond of = liking)
You should be ashamed of your behaviour!
(ashamed of = feeling shame about)

⭐ Key points

  1. -ed / -ing: -ed describes a person's feeling (interested); -ing describes what causes the feeling (interesting).
  2. afraid is for a specific fear; anxious is general worry; scared = frightened.
  3. Degrees of anger: cross (mild) < angry < furious (strongest, with lose your temper).
  4. Body language: nod your head = agree, shake your head = disagree; blush when embarrassed, yawn when tired or bored.
  5. Opinion phrases: in my opinion, as far as I'm concerned, to be honest, be keen on.
  6. Fixed prepositions: ashamed of, fond of, believe in, keen on, cross with, laugh at, fed up with.
