1. At the restaurant

bill — the piece of paper that shows what you must pay in a restaurant (not account or addition).

book a table — reserve a table in advance. menu — the list of dishes available. order — ask for food or drink to be brought to you.

dish — a particular kind of food; a plate is the flat object you eat from. "This dish is a speciality" is about the food, not the plate.

portion — an amount of food for one person; a helping is one serving put onto your plate.

main course — the largest part of a meal (after the starter, before the dessert). speciality — a dish a restaurant is famous for.

help yourself — take what you want: "Please help yourself to salads."

vegetarian — a person who never eats meat (a vegetable is a plant you eat; vegetation is all the plants in an area).

raw — not cooked at all (raw fish); undercooked — not cooked enough; crude — in a natural state (crude oil).

rare / well done — steak cooked very lightly / cooked thoroughly.

Waiter, could you bring me the bill, please?
(asking for the bill at the end of a meal)
It's a very popular restaurant — we should book a table.
This dish is a speciality of our restaurant.
(a speciality = the dish a restaurant is known for)
Paul never eats meat; he's a vegetarian.

2. Cooking and the kitchen

recipe — instructions for cooking a dish (a prescription is the paper a doctor gives you; ingredients are the things you cook with).

lay the table — put plates, glasses and cutlery on the table before a meal; clear away — remove the dishes after a meal; do the washing-up — wash the dishes.

warm up — heat food that has gone cold; boil over — (milk, water) bubble over the edge of the pan.

carving knife — a long knife for cutting thin slices of cooked meat. freshly ground coffee — coffee ground just before it is used.

Kitchen things: saucepan — a deep cooking pot with one long handle; frying pan — a flat, shallow pan; casserole dish — a dish with a lid, for cooking in the oven; kettle — for boiling water; jug — a container with a handle for pouring liquids; bowl; mug — a tall cup with a handle; tin opener — a tool for opening tins; food mixer — an electric machine for mixing food.

Fixed pairs: cups and saucers, fish and chips, knives and forks, bread and butter, eggs and bacon, salt and pepper. Amounts: a loaf of bread, a bottle of wine, a can of beer, a bar of chocolate, a jar of instant coffee, a bunch of grapes.

Dinner's nearly ready. Can you lay the table?
Keep an eye on the milk or it might boil over.
I really enjoyed that freshly ground coffee you made for me.
(freshly ground = ground a moment ago)

3. Phrasal verbs for food and cooking

cut down on — eat or use less of something (cut down on fatty food).

wear off — (a smell, an effect) gradually disappear.

look round — walk round a place, looking at things (look round the market).

work out — understand or find the answer to something (work out what it means).

feel up to — feel well or strong enough to do something.

put someone off something — make someone stop liking something; be put out — feel annoyed or offended.

get round to — finally do something you have been meaning to do for a while.

make up — invent something, such as a recipe, as you go along. leftovers — food remaining from an earlier meal.

I'm trying to cut down on fatty food.
It put me off eating meat for a week.
I haven't got round to cleaning the fish yet.
Keith usually makes up his recipes as he goes along.

⭐ Key points

  1. bill = what you pay in a restaurant; book a table = reserve a table; menu = the list of dishes; order = ask for food.
  2. dish = the food itself; plate = the flat dish you eat from; portion / helping = one person's amount; main course = the biggest part of the meal.
  3. raw = not cooked; undercooked = not cooked enough; rare = lightly cooked; well done = thoroughly cooked.
  4. Fixed pairs: fish and chips, bread and butter, salt and pepper, cups and saucers. Amounts: a loaf of, a bottle of, a can of, a bar of, a jar of, a bunch of.
  5. Phrasal verbs: cut down on, wear off, look round, work out, feel up to, put off, get round to, make up, warm up.
  6. Compounds that describe use: teaspoon, tablecloth, teapot, toaster, dishwasher.
