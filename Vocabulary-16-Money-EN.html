1. Earning, spending and borrowing

earn · charge · afford · spend · save · economise · borrow · lend · owe · debt · pay off · put aside

earn: get money for working; charge: ask an amount of money for something; spend: use money (or time); save: keep money for the future; economise: spend carefully. borrow means take something (from someone) for a time; lend means give it to them. afford is used with can/could: can't afford it / can't afford to buy it.

Money you must pay back involves owe and debt; to finish paying is to pay off a debt. To keep money regularly for a purpose is to put aside / put by money.

Could you lend me some money until Friday?
We can't afford to buy a new car this year.
I'm putting some money aside each week for my holidays.

2. Money opposites

generous ↔ mean · well off ↔ hard up · profit ↔ loss · prosperity ↔ poverty · valuable ↔ worthless · pay in ↔ take out · sale ↔ purchase

generous (happy to give) ↔ mean (not willing to spend); well off (rich enough) ↔ hard up (short of money); profit (money gained) ↔ loss (money lost); prosperity (wealth and success) ↔ poverty (being poor); valuable (worth a lot) ↔ worthless (worth nothing). At a bank: pay in (put money in) ↔ take out (remove money); trade seen from two sides: sale ↔ purchase. Paying: by cheque ↔ (in) cash.

Last year their business made a huge profit.
The old painting turned out to be worthless.

3. Banks, documents and people

pension · wealth · credit card · loan · receipt · tip · rent · safe · account · interest · savings · insurance · bill

pension: regular money paid to someone who has retired; wealth: a large amount of money or property; loan: money borrowed (usually from a bank); receipt: the piece of paper showing you paid; tip: extra money for a waiter or driver; rent: money paid to live in a house; safe: a strong metal box for valuables; interest: money the bank pays you on savings; insurance: protection you pay for against loss or illness.

heir · pensioner · accountant · customer · cashier · agent · investor · swindler

People: heir (inherits money or property), pensioner (receives a pension), accountant (keeps or checks financial records), customer (buys things in a shop), cashier (pays out money in a bank), agent (represents others in business), investor (puts money into a business), swindler (cheats people out of money).

David never carries cash with him and pays for everything by credit card.
If you put your money in the bank, it will earn ten per cent interest.
As his only heir, she inherited the whole fortune.

4. Expressions with pay

payments · pay back · pay for · pay out · unpaid · payable · pay into · well paid

payments: regular amounts paid; pay somebody back: return borrowed money; pay for + thing: give money for that thing; pay out: spend (a large amount); unpaid: not yet paid; payable to + name: who a cheque is written for — make the cheque payable to…; pay money into + account/bank: put money in; well paid: earning a good salary.

If you lend me the money, I'll pay you back next week.
Please make the cheque payable to R.D. Smith.
I must do something about all these unpaid bills.

⭐ Key points

  1. borrow = take for a time, lend = give for a time: borrow money from…, lend money to….
  2. afford only with can/could/be able to: can't afford it, can't afford to buy it.
  3. Paying: pay by credit card / by cheque but pay (in) cash; for the thing bought: pay for + thing.
  4. Cheques: sign a cheque, make a cheque payable to…; putting money in: pay money into….
  5. Opposite pairs to learn: generous↔mean, well off↔hard up, profit↔loss, valuable↔worthless, prosperity↔poverty.
  6. get in free = pay nothing to enter; a real bargain = something bought cheaply; money handed back when you pay is change.
