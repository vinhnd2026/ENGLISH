Travel vocabulary: transport and places · words for journeys · accommodation · compounds with sea-

1. Places and transport

a) On the boat and at the harbour

deck · cabin · harbour · quay · buffet

The deck is the open top part of a ship; a cabin is a small room for sleeping in on a ship; the harbour is the sheltered area where ships wait; the quay is the built platform beside the water where cars queue for a ferry; a buffet (buffet car) is the part of a train where you can buy food and drink.

b) Stations and airports

platform · coach station · departure lounge · runway · destination

A platform is where you get on a train; a coach station is the terminal for long-distance buses; the departure lounge is the waiting area at an airport; the runway is the strip a plane uses to take off and land; your destination is the place you are travelling to.

c) People and bags

crew · passengers · flight attendant · luggage · suitcase

The crew are the people who work on a ship or plane; passengers are the people travelling; a flight attendant looks after passengers on a plane; luggage is all your bags (an uncountable noun); a suitcase is one single case (countable).

d) Flights and tickets

flight · take off · land · delayed · cancelled · diverted · check in · declare · single · return · seat belt

A plane takes off when it leaves the ground and lands when it comes back down. A flight can be delayed (late), cancelled (not happening at all) or diverted (sent to a different airport). You check in before the flight and declare duty-free goods at customs. A single ticket is one-way; a return takes you there and back. Fasten your seat belt for take-off and landing.

The plane took off on time but arrived half an hour late.
You have to check in an hour before the plane leaves.
How much luggage can I take with me on the plane?

2. Words for journeys

These words all mean a kind of journey, but they differ in how you travel and why:

a cruise · a flight · a voyage · a crossing · a tour · a trip · an expedition · a package tour · an itinerary · travel

A cruise is a holiday journey on a ship, for pleasure; a flight is a journey by plane; a voyage is a long journey by sea; a crossing is a short sea journey from one side to the other; a tour is an organised journey to see the sights of a place; a trip is the general, informal word, often for a short journey; an expedition is a journey for a scientific or special purpose; a package tour is a holiday where travel and accommodation are arranged and paid for together; an itinerary is the plan of a journey; travel is the general idea of travelling (uncountable).

The Titanic sank on its first voyage in 1912.
Mr Dean is away on a business trip at the moment.
Travel is one of my main interests.

3. Accommodation

accommodation · a fortnight · double room · guest-house · camp-site · hostel · vacancy · book · hitch-hike · porter

Accommodation is the general word for a place to stay (uncountable). A fortnight is two weeks. A double room is a room for two people. A guest-house is a private house where you pay to stay and have meals. A camp-site is a place where you can put up a tent. A hostel is a cheap place for travellers or students to stay. A vacancy is a room that is free. To book means to reserve something in advance. To hitch-hike is to travel by getting free lifts in other people's cars. A porter carries your bags in a hotel or station.

I'd like to book three single rooms for next week, please.
We had no money so we had to hitch-hike.
I stayed in France for a fortnight last year.

4. Compound words with sea-

seaside · seafood · seaweed · sea level · sea front · seagulls · seasick · seashore

The seaside is the beach area where people go on holiday; seafood is food from the sea — fish, shrimps and so on; seaweed is the green plant that grows in the sea; sea level is the level of the sea, used to measure height; the sea front is the road or buildings facing the sea; seagulls are the white birds that live near the sea; if you feel seasick, travelling on a boat makes you feel ill; the seashore is the land right next to the sea.

Last year we didn't go to the mountains. We went to the seaside instead.
This town is very high up. It's a thousand metres above sea level.

⭐ Key points

  1. luggage and travel (in the general sense) are uncountable: do not say *a luggage or *a travel for one journey — use a trip / a journey instead.
  2. Choose the journey word by transport and purpose: flight (plane) · voyage (long, by sea) · crossing (short, across the sea) · cruise (for pleasure) · tour (sightseeing) · expedition (scientific/special) · trip (general).
  3. single = one-way; return = there and back. Do not confuse single with simple (easy/plain).
  4. check in (register for a flight) is not check up; take off (leave the ground) is not take up.
  5. book is both a noun (a thing you read) and a verb (to reserve) — the verb is the one you need for rooms and tickets.
  6. seasick = ill from boat travel; the same pattern gives carsick and airsick.
