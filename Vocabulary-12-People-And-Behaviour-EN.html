People and behaviour: Character adjectives · Word formation · Family & relationships · Phrasal verbs

1. Positive character adjectives

cheerful — happy and lively; generous — happy to give money or help; honest — truthful; reliable — someone you can trust to do what they promise; punctual — arriving on time; considerate — thinking about other people's needs; sympathetic — understanding other people's feelings; thoughtful — kind and thinking of others; patient — able to wait calmly; tolerant — accepting people as they are; frank — saying exactly what you think; determined — having a strong wish to succeed; ambitious — wanting to get to the top; brave — not afraid in danger; sociable — liking company; grateful — thankful; proud — feeling pleased about something you have or have done; kind — friendly and helpful; polite — having good manners; imaginative — having new and clever ideas.

Learn them through definitions: You do what you say you will do → reliable; You say exactly what you think → frank; You think about the needs of others → considerate; You understand the feelings of others → sympathetic; You always arrive on time → punctual.

Tom always pays for everyone when we go out. He's so generous.
Our teacher is very strict and won't let us talk in class.
You can always count on Anna — she is conscientious and punctual.

2. Negative character adjectives

rude — not polite; mean — not willing to spend money or share, also unkind; lazy — avoiding work; shy — nervous with other people; jealous — unhappy because someone has what you want; aggressive — angry and ready to argue or fight; selfish — thinking only about yourself; bad-tempered — becoming angry easily; stubborn — refusing to change your mind; snobbish — looking down on other people; greedy — wanting more than you need.

Useful opposite pairs: honest ↔ dishonest, polite ↔ impolite / rude, friendly ↔ unfriendly, generous ↔ mean, patient ↔ impatient, obedient ↔ disobedient.

Please don't push. It's very rude.

3. Word formation for character

Verb + -ing makes an adjective: annoy → annoying. Negative prefixes: disobedient (dis-), impolite (im-), unfriendly, dishonest. talk → talkative (with -ative).

Abstract nouns: proud → pride, brave → bravery, violent → violence, conscience → conscientious (careful and thorough in your work).

4. Family, relationships and phrasal verbs

People words: nephew — the son of your brother or sister; niece — the daughter of your brother or sister; bride — a woman on her wedding day; fiancé — the man a woman is engaged to; twin — one of two children born at the same time; neighbour — a person who lives near you; adult — a fully grown person; widow — a woman whose husband has died; best man — the bridegroom's friend at a wedding; sister-in-law — the sister of the person you marry; colleague — a person you work with; toddler — a young child learning to walk.

Phrasal verbs: turn up — arrive, appear; look up to — respect; put up with — tolerate; get on well with — have a good relationship with; call in — visit for a short time on your way somewhere; count on — rely on; give away — give something free; fall out — quarrel; turn down — refuse; take after — look or behave like an older member of your family.

Fixed phrases: in love; go out with — have a romantic relationship with; as if — as though; break up — come to an end (a marriage, a relationship); be related to — belong to the same family as; be brought up — be raised; kind of you — a way of thanking someone; patient with; disappointed with; out of character — not typical of someone; in private — away from other people.

The son of your brother or sister is your nephew; the daughter is your niece.
The bride failed to turn up, so the wedding was cancelled.
My brother and I really get on well with our parents.
After a few days they realised they were in love.
I was brought up by my aunt after my parents died.

⭐ Key points

  1. Useful opposite pairs: honest ↔ dishonest, polite ↔ impolite/rude, friendly ↔ unfriendly, obedient ↔ disobedient, generous ↔ mean.
  2. mean has two senses: not willing to spend money or share (stingy), and unkind.
  3. Prepositions: proud of, disappointed with, patient with, related to; It was kind of you is a way of thanking someone.
  4. Word building: annoy → annoying; negative prefixes dis-/im-/un- (disobedient, impolite, unfriendly, dishonest); nouns pride (from proud), bravery (from brave), violence (from violent), conscientious (from conscience).
  5. Family: nephew (a brother's or sister's son) ↔ niece (daughter); a fiancé is the man a woman is engaged to; the best man is the bridegroom's friend at a wedding; a sister-in-law is the sister of the person you marry; a widow is a woman whose husband has died.
  6. Phrasal verbs: turn up (appear), look up to (respect), put up with (tolerate), get on well with (have a good relationship with), call in (visit briefly), count on (rely on), give away (give free), fall out (quarrel), turn down (refuse), take after (resemble a family member).
