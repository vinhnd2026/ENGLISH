Grammar 14 — Time Expressions

Time expressions: Nowadays · These days · In / On / At · For / Since / Ago · By / Until · On time / In time · At the end / In the end / At last

1. Talking about the present and the future

a) Nowadays / these days — the present contrasted with the past

Both expressions set the present in contrast with the past: “nowadays”, “these days”.

Nowadays more women have careers.
What are you up to these days?
⚠️ Actually does not carry a time meaning, as it does in some other European languages; actually means “in fact, really”.

b) In + a period of time — pointing to the future

In can refer to future time: it points to the end point of a period of time measured from now.

in + a period of time → at the end of that period
I'll be there in a moment.
I'll be back in a week.

c) Counting by the calendar (suppose today is Monday)

Wednesday is the day after tomorrow or the day after next, and can also be described as in two days' time. Next Monday is this time next week.

Wednesday is in two days' time.

d) At the end / in the end / at last

At the end points to a final moment of something. In the end means “finally, after a long time” (= eventually). At last also means “finally”, but with a feeling of relief that the waiting is over.

I left at the end of the film.
We waited for ages, and in the end we left.
At last you have arrived!

e) Presently

Presently means “very soon, in a moment” — a formal use.

Just a minute. I'll be with you presently.

2. Talking about the past, and pairs that are easily confused

a) Once — two meanings

Once can mean “at some time in the past, formerly” (past), or “as soon as, from the moment that” (looking forward from a point).

I lived here once.
Once she gets here, we can leave.

b) Last goes only with night

Last can be used with night, but not with morning / afternoon / evening — for those parts of the day, use yesterday.

They left yesterday morning and came back last night.

If today is Monday, then last Saturday night is the night before last.

c) In those days / at that time

Both refer back to a past time that has already been mentioned.

I met Janet in 1980. In those days I was a keen dancer.

d) After / later / afterwards

After must be followed by an object. Later means “a short time after” and takes no object. Afterwards means “after something has happened”.

I'll see you after the holidays.
I'm busy now. I'll call you back later.
The film is starting now. I'll tell you afterwards.

e) In / on / at with time expressions

in + month / year / part of the day  ·  on + day  ·  at + clock time / point in time

In January, in 1968, in the morning/afternoon/evening; on Thursday, on my birthday; at six o'clock, at night, at midday/midnight, at the weekend.

f) For / since / ago

for + a period of time  ·  since + a point in time  ·  ago = back from now
I lived there for ten years.
(for + a period of time)
I have lived here since 1999.
(since + the starting point)
Edward died two years ago.
(ago = how far back)

g) By / until

By = at the latest, at or before that time. Until = continuously up to that time.

I need your homework by next week.
I'll be away until next week.

h) On time / in time

On time = punctual, not late. In time = early enough to do something.

Bill is never late, he's always on time.
Luckily the police arrived in time to help.

⭐ Key points

  1. Nowadays / these days: the present in contrast with the past. Actually has no time meaning — it means “in fact”.
  2. In + a period of time can point to the future: the end point of that period (I'll be back in a week).
  3. At the end (the final point of something) ≠ in the end (finally, = eventually) ≠ at last (finally — with relief that the waiting is over).
  4. Last goes only with night: last night, but yesterday morning / afternoon / evening.
  5. After must be followed by an object; later and afterwards are not.
  6. For + a period of time; since + the starting point; ago goes with the past simple and says how long before now something happened.
  7. By = at the latest by that time; until = continuing right up to that time.
  8. On time = punctual; in time = early enough.
