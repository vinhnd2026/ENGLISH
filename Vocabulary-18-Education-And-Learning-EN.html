Education and learning: confused pairs · people in education · classroom verbs · do/make/take · word formation · preposition phrases

1. Easily confused pairs at school

Many education words come in similar pairs — keep them clearly apart:

course (the whole series of lessons) · lesson (one meeting) · marks (school results) · points (in games and competitions) · study · learn · teach · train (for a job) · fail an exam · miss an exam (not be there) · go in for an examination (enter for it) · raise your hand · rise (go up by itself) · by heart (from memory) · term (part of the school year)
Sheila always got good marks in algebra.
(marks, not points, for school work)
In history we had to learn a lot of dates by heart.
(learn by heart = memorize)
I hope your work will improve by the end of term.
(term = one part of the school year)

2. People in education

Words for people, grouped by their role — learn them with their definitions:

lecturer (teaches at a university) · graduate (has a college degree) · principal (head of a school) · pupil (at primary or secondary school) · professor (the most important teacher in a university department) · tutor (teaches one student or a very small class) · classmate (in the same class as you) · coach (trains a sports team) · examiner (writes the exam question papers) · learner (someone learning — including a driver who has not passed the test yet)
Someone who teaches one student or a very small class is a tutor.
Edward has a degree in French from Leeds University.
(a degree in + subject)

3. Classroom verbs & do / make / take

Common verbs in learning contexts:

punish · divide · memorise · pay (attention) · pass · copy · concentrate · revise · underline · cheat

The verbs do / make / take combine with fixed nouns — they cannot be translated word for word:

do an exercise · do well · do your best · make progress · make a guess · make errors · take an exam · take a break · take notes
If you divide twenty-seven by nine, the answer is three.
(divide … by …)
Paul finds maths difficult, but he does his best.
(do your best = try as hard as you can)

4. Word formation & preposition phrases

The word family from the school report:

INTRODUCE → introduction · SUCCESS → successful · ATTEND → attention · READ → unreadable · FAIL → failure · REVISE → revision · MISTAKE → mistaken · ABSENT → absences · PUNISH → punishments · GIFT → gifted

The first-letter words (Exercise 8):

knowledge · discipline · education · dictionary · calculator · university · intelligence · comprehension · secondary · experiments

Fixed preposition phrases:

put up your hand · pay attention to · a degree in · be punished for · be good at · multiply … by … · a composition about/on · write in your exercise books · learn by heart · pick up a language
Mary has a degree in civil engineering.
When Sue visited Italy, she soon picked up the language.
(pick up = learn naturally, without formal study)

⭐ Key points

  1. course = the whole series of lessons; lesson = one meeting. marks are for school work; points are for games and competitions.
  2. fail an exam = not pass it; miss an exam = not be there for it. Go in for an examination = enter for it.
  3. raise needs an object (raise your hand); rise goes up by itself, with no object.
  4. Fixed combinations: do an exercise, do your best; make progress, make a guess, make mistakes; take an exam, take a break, take notes.
  5. Teachers: lecturer (university), tutor (one-to-one), principal (head of a school), professor (head of a department), examiner (writes the exam papers).
  6. Fixed phrases: pay attention to, a degree in, good at, learn by heart, pick up a language.
