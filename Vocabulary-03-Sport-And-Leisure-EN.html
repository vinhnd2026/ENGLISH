1. Sports equipment

Each sport has its own equipment — learn the words in sport + equipment pairs:

costume (swimming costume) · saddle (for a horse) · club (golf) · net (tennis) · handlebars (bicycle) · rope (climbing) · ice (skating) · gloves (boxing) · whistle (referee) · glasses (dark glasses for skiing) · racket · rod (fishing)

Suddenly the referee blew his whistle and pointed to the penalty spot.
Diana's bike crashed into a tree, and she was thrown over the handlebars.
Terry went fishing with the new rod his parents gave him.

2. Games and hobbies

cards · draughts (a board game with pieces) · billiards · crossword · embroidery (sewing pictures with thread) · model-making · hiking (long walks in the country) · gambling (playing games for money)

I couldn't do ten down, so I used the dictionary.
a crossword clue: “ten down” is a vertical answer
When Ellen is losing, she knocks the pieces off the board.
Collecting matchboxes is Rebecca's favourite pastime.
a pastime is something you do for pleasure in your free time

3. Competing and results

race · ground (a sports field/stadium) · fit (healthy and strong) · spectators (people watching at the ground) · draw (a game that ends with equal scores) · prize · competitors · pastime

beat + the opponent (person/team)  ·  win + a match / competition / prize
⚠️ Careful: beat is followed by the opponent (beat the other team), while win is followed by the match, competition or prize (win the match, win first prize). You cannot say win a team.
Our team won the match by two goals to nil.
Brenda goes jogging every morning to keep fit.
The final result was a draw.

Replacement words used in the exercises: professionally · postponed (put off to another time) · champion · score · arranged (fixed for a date) · spare time (free time) · second (the runner-up position) · captain · record (the best ever performance) · referee · outdoors · side (a team)

4. Phrases with get

get better (recover) · get stuck · get ready · get lost · get anywhere (make progress — usually negative) · get the dinner (cook it) · get back (return) · get it right · get off (a horse/bike) · get through (on the phone) · get together (meet) · get used to

When you rewrite a sentence without get, replace the phrase with a single direct verb of the same meaning: recover, become stuck, prepare, lose one's way, succeed, cook, arrive home, perfect, dismount, meet…

I can't get used to playing football on plastic grass!
We have a great time whenever our rugby team gets together.

5. Word formation

Prefixes and suffixes in this unit: -ful → skilful, successful · -ive → imaginative · -able → valuable, unbelievable · un- (negative) → unexpected, unbelievable · mis- → misunderstanding · dis- → disagreement · nouns: photography, failure.

Just then I had an unexpected piece of good luck.
She said she could sell me a valuable camera.

⭐ Key points

  1. Equipment by sport: racket (tennis), club (golf), rod (fishing), saddle (riding), whistle (referee).
  2. beat + the opponent; win + a match/competition/prize. An equal result is a draw.
  3. keep fit = stay healthy; spectators = the people watching at the ground.
  4. Phrases with get: get used to + -ing, get through (by phone), get together (meet), get better (recover), get lost.
  5. Word formation: negative prefixes un-/mis-/dis-; adjective suffixes -ful, -ive, -able.
